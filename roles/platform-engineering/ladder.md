# Platform Engineer (IC Technical, E1-E6) - Canonical — Career Ladder

Canonical six-level IC ladder for platform engineering, consolidated by union from five independent same-methodology generations; E3 Senior is the terminal level.


| Level | Title | Scope | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Associate Platform Engineer | task | Learns the platform and ships well-defined tasks with guidance |
| E2 | Platform Engineer | component | Owns platform components end to end and unblocks internal users independently |
| E3 | Senior Platform Engineer | capability / domain (terminal level) | Owns a platform capability as a product; terminal level |
| E4 | Staff Platform Engineer | multiple teams | Shapes platform architecture and adoption across multiple teams |
| E5 | Senior Staff Platform Engineer | organization | Sets platform strategy and standards for the engineering organization |
| E6 | Principal Platform Engineer | company | Sets company-wide platform direction and technical bets |

## Platform as a Product

#### Product Discovery & Feedback

##### Internal user research

*Anchor:* Noda, Storey, Forsgren & Greiler, DevEx: What Actually Drives Productivity, ACM Queue 21(2) (2023) — grounds discovery in evidence about how developers actually experience the platform, not provider assumptions.

- **E1 (= P1)** — Runs scripted customer interviews and notes findings under a senior researcher's guidance. *Evidenced by:* Shadows platform users and sits in on interviews and support rotations, writing up the friction they hit. *Scope:* task. *Why this level:* PM-01 P1 ('runs scripted customer interviews and notes findings under a senior researcher's guidance') is the guided-task bar an Associate operates at -- they sit in on interviews and write up friction, they do not yet run discovery. (Noda, Storey, Forsgren & Greiler, DevEx: What Actually Drives Productivity, ACM Queue 21(2) (2023); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E2 (= P2)** — Plans and conducts standard discovery for a feature, synthesizing themes with normal review. *Evidenced by:* Runs structured interviews and support-channel reviews with the internal engineers using their component. *Scope:* component. *Why this level:* P2 ('plans and conducts standard discovery for a feature, synthesizing themes with normal review') matches a Platform Engineer running structured interviews for the one component they own, with review as the independence check. (Noda, Storey, Forsgren & Greiler, DevEx: What Actually Drives Productivity, ACM Queue 21(2) (2023); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E3 (= P3)** — Independently frames ambiguous problems, selects fitting methods, and separates signal from noise. *Evidenced by:* Designs and runs the discovery process for a capability. *Scope:* capability / domain (terminal level). *Why this level:* P3 ('independently frames ambiguous problems, selects fitting methods, and separates signal from noise') is what owning discovery for a whole capability requires -- no senior researcher frames the problem for them anymore. E3 is this ladder's terminal level (scope_note): sustained P3 mastery here is a complete career, not a waypoint -- consistent with how SFIA treats independent full-competency practice as a legitimate long-run professional register and with StaffEng's own point that not every strong senior engineer needs to become staff. (Noda, Storey, Forsgren & Greiler, DevEx: What Actually Drives Productivity, ACM Queue 21(2) (2023); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E4 (= P4)** — Designs the team's discovery approach and untangles findings others find contradictory. *Evidenced by:* Synthesizes research across several product teams into shared personas and friction maps other platform teams adopt. *Scope:* multiple teams. *Why this level:* P4 ('designs the team's discovery approach and untangles findings others find contradictory') is Staff-scope: the deliverable is a reusable discovery method other platform teams adopt, not one more interview round. (Noda, Storey, Forsgren & Greiler, DevEx: What Actually Drives Productivity, ACM Queue 21(2) (2023); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

(Noda, Storey, Forsgren & Greiler, DevEx: What Actually Drives Productivity, ACM Queue 21(2) (2023); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **E6 (= P6)** — Defines what rigorous product discovery means and shapes the discipline's practice externally. *Evidenced by:* Represents the internal developer experience at company level. *Scope:* company. *Why this level:* P6 ('defines what rigorous product discovery means and shapes the discipline's practice externally') is the only DevEx-research bar with company-plus-external reach. (Noda, Storey, Forsgren & Greiler, DevEx: What Actually Drives Productivity, ACM Queue 21(2) (2023); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

#### Platform Strategy & Scope

##### Thinnest viable platform

*Anchor:* Skelton & Pais, Team Topologies (IT Revolution, 2019) — defines the Thinnest Viable Platform -- the sizing test every build-buy-adopt and retirement decision here is judged against.

| OCF | E1 | E2 | E3 | E4 | E5 | E6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [OPS-31](../../data/capabilities.md#ops-31) — Thinnest viable platform scoping | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **E1 (= P1)** — Checks for an existing tool, service, or open-source option before writing new code and raises what they find; asks whether a proposed task serves a real user need. *Evidenced by:* Finds that an existing internal CLI already covers most of a proposed new tool's use case and raises it in planning instead of starting a new build. *Scope:* task. *Why this level:* OPS-31 P1 ('checks for an existing tool, service, or open-source option before writing new code ... asks whether a proposed task serves a real user need') is written at task scope -- one build decision, not a portfolio of them. (Skelton & Pais, Team Topologies (IT Revolution, 2019); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E2 (= P2)** — Proposes wrapping or configuring an existing service instead of building new when it meets the need, writing the comparison down and defending removal of unused features in their own component. *Evidenced by:* Recommends adopting the org's existing secrets-manager service instead of building a bespoke wrapper for their component, and writes up the build-vs-adopt comparison that settles it. *Scope:* component. *Why this level:* P2 ('proposes wrapping or configuring an existing service instead of building new when it meets the need ... defending removal of unused features in their own component') names 'their own component' verbatim -- the E2 band. (Skelton & Pais, Team Topologies (IT Revolution, 2019); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E3 (= P3)** — Keeps a whole capability at its thinnest viable size - makes explicit, documented build-buy-adopt decisions, ships the thinnest version that serves proven demand, and retires surface area whose maintenance cost exceeds its value. *Evidenced by:* Retires half a capability's plugin surface after usage telemetry shows only two of eight plugins are load-bearing, documenting the build-buy-adopt call for what stays. *Scope:* capability / domain (terminal level). *Why this level:* P3 ('keeps a whole capability at its thinnest viable size -- makes explicit, documented build-buy-adopt decisions ... retires surface area whose maintenance cost exceeds its value') names 'a whole capability' verbatim, and the terminal register: sustained thinness discipline over one capability is a complete job. E3 is this ladder's terminal level (scope_note): sustained P3 mastery here is a complete career, not a waypoint -- consistent with how SFIA treats independent full-competency practice as a legitimate long-run professional register and with StaffEng's own point that not every strong senior engineer needs to become staff. (Skelton & Pais, Team Topologies (IT Revolution, 2019); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E4 (= P4)** — Arbitrates overlap and sprawl across teams - merges duplicate offerings, cuts under-used surface, and sets the bar for what deserves to be shared platform at all. *Evidenced by:* Merges two platform teams' overlapping feature-flag services into one and retires the duplicate. *Scope:* multiple teams. *Why this level:* P4 ('arbitrates overlap and sprawl across teams -- merges duplicate offerings ... sets the bar for what deserves to be shared platform at all') names 'across teams' verbatim -- E4. (Skelton & Pais, Team Topologies (IT Revolution, 2019); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **E5 (= P5)** — Shapes the organization's platform boundary - what the platform owns versus product teams versus vendors - and drives the organization to shed what it should not own. *Evidenced by:* Draws the line for what the platform organization owns versus what product teams should just buy, and gets teams to hand back components that crossed it. *Scope:* organization. *Why this level:* P5 ('shapes the organization's platform boundary -- what the platform owns versus product teams versus vendors -- and drives the organization to shed what it should not own') names 'the organization' verbatim -- E5. (Skelton & Pais, Team Topologies (IT Revolution, 2019); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **E6 (= P6)** — Sets the build-buy-adopt principles an entire company references, keeping company-scale platform investment proportional to the delivery it unlocks. *Evidenced by:* Kills a company-wide platform initiative whose maintenance cost has outgrown the delivery it unlocks, over objections that the build already happened. *Scope:* company. *Why this level:* P6 ('sets the build-buy-adopt principles an entire company references, keeping company-scale platform investment proportional to the delivery it unlocks') names company scale verbatim -- E6. (Skelton & Pais, Team Topologies (IT Revolution, 2019); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

- **E2 (= P2)** — Influences component-level decisions through credibility. *Evidenced by:* Proposes direction within their component. *Scope:* component. *Why this level:* P2 ('influences component-level decisions through credibility') names 'component-level' verbatim -- E2. - **E3 (= P3)** — Sets technical direction for a domain; others align to their calls. *Evidenced by:* Picks the message-queue technology a capability standardizes on for its next migration cycle, and engineers building on it align their designs to that call without relitigating it. *Scope:* capability / domain (terminal level). *Why this level:* P3 ('sets technical direction for a domain; others align to their calls') names 'a domain' verbatim, and the terminal register: a capability's technical direction is a complete, standing mandate, not a stepping stone. - **E4 (= P4)** — Shapes how multiple teams approach problems; trusted tiebreaker. *Evidenced by:* Aligns multiple teams behind a shared technical direction without authority over them. *Scope:* multiple teams. *Why this level:* P4 ('shapes how multiple teams approach problems; trusted tiebreaker') names 'multiple teams' verbatim -- E4. (Nygard, Documenting Architecture Decisions (Cognitect blog, 2011); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **E5 (= P5)** — Influences org-wide technical strategy. *Evidenced by:* Writes the organization's technical strategy and makes it operational. *Scope:* organization. *Why this level:* P5 ('influences org-wide technical strategy') names 'org-wide' verbatim -- E5. (Nygard, Documenting Architecture Decisions (Cognitect blog, 2011); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **E6 (= P6)** — Influences company strategic direction through insight. *Evidenced by:* Sets company-level technical direction. *Scope:* company. *Why this level:* P6 ('influences company strategic direction through insight') names 'company' verbatim -- E6. (Nygard, Documenting Architecture Decisions (Cognitect blog, 2011); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
