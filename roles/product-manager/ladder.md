# Product Manager - Canonical Career Ladder

## Calibration summary

- **Provenance:** canonical consolidation of five independent sources by **union, not intersection** - every genuinely distinct competency from any source survives; nothing was voted out. Sources: four blind same-methodology runs (pm-r1 ... pm-r4, 126 source competencies) plus one trusted different-lineage matrix (the Muller PM skill matrix, 26 rows; its competency territory was used as union evidence while its prose was rewritten to the observable-behavior register and genericized). 152 source rows consolidated into 40 canonical competencies. Semantic duplicates were merged into single canonical competencies (clearest name under 60 characters, strongest citable anchor from the product canon, best observable cell content). Minted 2026-07-02.
- **Variant:** ic_technical (individual-contributor product track - the maker track applied to the product craft; upper levels are craft leverage and adoption breadth, never headcount).
- **Levels:** six, P1-P6 - Associate Product Manager -> Product Manager -> Senior Product Manager -> Staff/Group Product Manager -> Principal Product Manager -> Distinguished PM / Head of Product Craft. **Note on codes:** the ladder's level codes P1-P6 are the column labels of this role and are distinct from the catalog's P1-P6 proficiency scale; each competency's OCF table shows the proficiency target (linked to the proficiency scale) under each level column.
- **Scope bands:** one feature/epic (P1) -> a product area (P2) -> a product/capability (P3, terminal) -> multiple product areas (P4) -> product organization (P5) -> company portfolio (P6). Scope widens through adoption breadth, not headcount.
- **Terminal level: P3 / Senior Product Manager.** All five sources agree in structure: a strong PM can remain at P3 indefinitely without being behind. P4+ is not "more of P3" - it is a different job defined by multi-area scope and craft leverage across PMs the person does not manage.
- **Register:** CircleCI-style present-tense observable behaviors; Depth and Scope separated in every cell; upper levels written as influence/adoption behaviors demonstrable before holding the title.
- **Shape:** 7 key areas, 17 focus areas, 40 competencies; every focus area spans 2+ competencies; theme labels are under 60 characters and organization-agnostic.
- **OCF mapping:** every competency carries an Open Capability Framework reference - 33 map to existing catalog capabilities (all 14 PM-domain capabilities are used, plus PD, CC, LI ids, including the newly minted CC-06 executive communication and PD-07 strategic & commercial awareness) and 7 rows ride on 5 proposed additions (PM-15 ... PM-19 candidates) staged under contrib/. PM-01 (three rows), PM-10 (three rows), PM-04, PM-06, PM-09 (two rows each) and the PM-18 candidate (three rows) are flagged as future split candidates rather than forcing weaker distinct mappings.
- **Consolidation judgment calls:** (1) Blended source rows were assigned to the concept their cell content leans toward: r1's "Opportunity mapping & synthesis" to Opportunity mapping; r1's and r3's "Product vision and strategy" to Product strategy formulation; r3's "Problem statements and JTBD framing" to Problem statements & outcome framing; r2's "Launch & post-launch iteration" to Launch management & readiness; r2's "Business model & pricing awareness" to Business model & unit economics; r1's "Alignment, expectations & saying no" and r3's "Stakeholder alignment and saying no" to Stakeholder alignment & expectations; Muller's "Strategy definition and goal setting" to Product strategy formulation and "Direct people management & recruiting" to Hiring & interviewing PMs (its mentoring half is fully covered by the four-run Mentoring cluster). (2) r3's "Validation decisions: persevere, pivot, kill" merged into Post-launch iteration & lifecycle, whose cells carry the persevere/pivot/kill bar at P3. (3) r1's split of demand-side assumption testing from prototype/solution testing survives under the union rule: Assumption testing & experiments and Prototype & solution testing are separate rows; the other runs' blended rows were assigned to Assumption testing & experiments. (4) Muller-only territory survives as four competencies with fresh cells written to the register: Customer segmentation & personas (PM-02), Technical & design fluency (LI-06), Hiring & interviewing PMs (LI-02), and Muller's agile/backlog rows folded into Backlog & delivery flow. (6) Proficiency targets follow the IC guide P1:P1 ... P6:P6 for every row except Technical & design fluency (LI-06), capped at {P4: P3, P5: P4, P6: P4}: a PM's leverage at P4+ comes through product craft, not deeper technical authority, and the source cells plateau accordingly.

## Level overview

| Level | Title | Scope band | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Associate Product Manager | one feature/epic | Learns the craft; ships well-defined slices with guidance |
| P2 | Product Manager | a product area | Runs discovery-to-delivery independently for an area |
| P3 | Senior Product Manager | a product/capability - **terminal level** | Owns outcomes for a product; sets the bar others copy |
| P4 | Staff/Group Product Manager | multiple product areas | Aligns strategy and craft across several product teams |
| P5 | Principal Product Manager | product organization | Sets product direction and practice the org builds against |
| P6 | Distinguished PM / Head of Product Craft | company portfolio | Shapes portfolio bets and the company's product craft |

**Terminal level - P3 / Senior Product Manager.** Sustained excellence at P3 is a complete, respected career, not a waypoint: full ownership of a product or capability, the craft bar for their team, trusted with contested calls. Progression beyond P3 is opt-in and changes the nature of the job - from owning a product to aligning the people, strategies, and practices around many products.

## Competency matrix

Each cell separates **Depth** (mastery of the skill) from **Scope** (how far the work reaches). Cells are present-tense behaviors observable by peers, leaders, and stakeholders.

## Product Discovery & User Research

### Continuous Discovery

#### Customer interviewing

*Anchor:* Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits* (2021); Portigal, *Interviewing Users* (2013) - weekly customer touchpoints with story-based, non-leading questions are the foundational discovery habit. *Why:* everything downstream - opportunities, bets, roadmaps - inherits the quality of the raw customer contact.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [PM-01](../../data/capabilities.md#pm-01) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **Runs customer interviews from a prepared story-based guide and captures verbatim notes**, asking follow-up questions that pull specific past behavior rather than opinions; debriefs findings with their lead. Scope: interviews recruited for one feature or epic.
- **P2** - Depth: **Maintains a weekly interviewing cadence without being told to**, writing their own discussion guides, avoiding leading questions, and turning each session into snapshots teammates actually read. Scope: continuous touchpoints across their product area.
- **P3** - Depth: **Runs the interviewing practice for their product** - builds the recruiting pipeline, brings engineers and designers into sessions, and redirects interviews that drift into feature-request collection back to behavior. Scope: their product/capability; teammates cite their interview snapshots in decisions.
- **P4** - Depth: **Teaches interviewing so visibly that other teams' guides and snapshot formats trace back to them**; sits in on other PMs' sessions and gives technique-level feedback. Scope: interviewing practice across multiple product areas.
- **P5** - Depth: **Installs the org's interviewing infrastructure** - recruiting panels, consent and incentive norms, snapshot repositories - and audits whether teams' customer contact is real or theater. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Sets the company's standard for customer contact** and argues it externally in talks or writing that practitioners cite. Scope: company portfolio; executives quote customer evidence in the register this person established.

#### Discovery cadence & habits

*Anchor:* Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits* (2021); Cagan, *Inspired* (2nd ed., 2018) - discovery is a weekly habit run in parallel with delivery, not a phase before it. *Why:* the cadence itself is observable, and its absence is the leading indicator of a build-trap team.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [PM-01](../../data/capabilities.md#pm-01) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **Keeps discovery activities visible on the team board** - interviews scheduled, assumptions listed, findings logged - and follows the cadence the team has set. Scope: their epic's discovery tasks.
- **P2** - Depth: **Runs discovery and delivery in parallel without stalling either** - the team ships weekly and talks to customers weekly, and standups show both tracks moving. Scope: the discovery rhythm of their area.
- **P3** - Depth: **Sustains the discovery habit through pressure** - deadlines, exec pushes, re-orgs - and visibly renegotiates scope rather than silently dropping customer contact. Scope: a product's discovery culture; their cadence is the example cited to new teams.
- **P4** - Depth: **Audits discovery health across teams from artifacts and calendars**, names which teams have quietly stopped learning, and gets the habit restarted. Scope: multiple product areas.
- **P5** - Depth: **Designs the operating rhythm that makes discovery the default** - planning rituals, review gates, and funding rules that assume continuous learning. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Holds the company accountable to discovery at the portfolio level**, challenging major investments that lack recent customer evidence in front of executive audiences. Scope: company portfolio.

### Synthesis & Customer Modeling

#### Opportunity mapping

*Anchor:* Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits* (2021) - the opportunity solution tree keeps teams solving customer needs rather than shipping their first idea. *Why:* mapping opportunities before solutions is the observable discipline that separates discovery from feature-taking.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [proposed](../../contrib/2026-07-opportunity-mapping-framing.md) (PM-15 candidate) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **Sorts interview evidence into needs, pain points, and desires on an existing opportunity map**, distinguishing an opportunity from a solution when asked to label one. Scope: contributes nodes to a map their lead owns.
- **P2** - Depth: **Builds and maintains the opportunity solution tree for their area**, sizing branches with evidence counts and pruning opportunities no interview supports. Scope: their product area; the tree is the artifact planning meetings open with.
- **P3** - Depth: **Frames the target opportunity space against the product outcome** and defends why one branch is being pursued over its siblings, naming the evidence and the trade-off. Scope: their product; adjacent teams borrow the map to see where their work intersects.
- **P4** - Depth: **Reconciles opportunity maps across teams into a shared structure**, exposing duplicate bets and gaps between areas that no single team could see. Scope: multiple product areas.
- **P5** - Depth: **Makes opportunity mapping the org's default synthesis method**, reviewing trees in strategy checkpoints and rejecting solution-first proposals that skip the structure. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Shapes how the company represents customer needs at portfolio level**, connecting opportunity structures to corporate strategy in artifacts the executive team plans against. Scope: company portfolio.

#### Research synthesis & insight

*Anchor:* Olsen, *The Lean Product Playbook* (2015) - the product-market fit pyramid depends on translating raw user input into underserved needs before designing anything. *Why:* synthesis is where raw data becomes decisions; done badly it launders anecdote into strategy.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [PM-01](../../data/capabilities.md#pm-01) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **Tags and clusters interview notes into themes using the team's synthesis method**, and can point to the specific quotes behind each theme. Scope: synthesis for one feature's research.
- **P2** - Depth: **Turns a batch of interviews, tickets, and usage data into a ranked set of underserved needs** with the supporting evidence attached, and presents it without over-claiming. Scope: synthesis their area's decisions cite.
- **P3** - Depth: **Integrates qualitative and quantitative evidence into insight documents that change what the team builds**; peers checking their synthesis find the chain from quote to claim intact. Scope: insight base for a product; other teams cite their write-ups.
- **P4** - Depth: **Spots contradictions between teams' research conclusions and forces reconciliation** - same customers, conflicting claims - until the shared evidence base is consistent. Scope: multiple product areas share one coherent picture of the customer.
- **P5** - Depth: **Builds the org's insight infrastructure** - repositories, tagging conventions, and synthesis reviews - so findings compound across teams instead of evaporating per project. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Distills company-wide customer understanding into the few durable insights executives and boards reason from**, and retires folklore that no longer survives the evidence. Scope: company portfolio.

#### Customer segmentation & personas

*Anchor:* Cooper, *The Inmates Are Running the Asylum* (1999) - personas as specific design targets that end "the elastic user"; Moore, *Crossing the Chasm* (3rd ed., 2014) - adoption is won segment by segment. *Why:* a product built for "everyone" is targeted by accident; segment and persona choices decide whose problem gets solved first.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [PM-02](../../data/capabilities.md#pm-02) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **Uses the team's personas and segments correctly in specs and interview recruiting** - names which persona a story serves, and flags work that serves no one in particular. Scope: one epic's user definitions.
- **P2** - Depth: **Keeps their area's personas and segments evidence-based** - refreshes them from interview and usage data, splits a segment when behavior diverges, and retires personas that no longer match observed users. Scope: their product area's segmentation.
- **P3** - Depth: **Chooses the target segment for their product deliberately and defends it** - names who the product is not for, sizes segments with data, and maps the end-to-end journey per segment so gaps become visible. Scope: their product; design and marketing build from their segment definitions.
- **P4** - Depth: **Reconciles segment and persona models across teams** so the portfolio addresses one coherent map of users rather than overlapping caricatures. Scope: multiple product areas.
- **P5** - Depth: **Owns the org's customer model** - the shared segmentation the org plans, staffs, and measures against - and audits products for segment drift. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Defines how the company sees its market's customers** - the segment map used in company strategy and board materials - and updates it ahead of market shifts. Scope: company portfolio.

## Problem Definition & Validation

### Problem Framing

#### Problem statements & outcome framing

*Anchor:* Perri, *Escaping the Build Trap* (2018) - teams stuck shipping outputs need problems framed as customer/business outcomes; Cagan, *Inspired* (2nd ed., 2018) - give teams problems to solve, not features to build. *Why:* a mis-framed problem wastes an entire delivery cycle no matter how well the rest is executed.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [proposed](../../contrib/2026-07-problem-framing-outcome-definition.md) (PM-16 candidate) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **Writes a one-page problem statement naming the user, the struggle, and the evidence** - and separates the problem from the requested feature when a stakeholder hands them a solution. Scope: one epic.
- **P2** - Depth: **Frames work as outcomes the team can move** ("reduce time-to-first-value") rather than outputs to ship, and rewrites incoming feature requests into the underlying problem before triage. Scope: their product area's backlog.
- **P3** - Depth: **Sets the outcome their product is accountable for and keeps the team pointed at it**, publicly re-framing efforts that drift into output-counting. Scope: their product; the problem framing survives their absence in planning docs.
- **P4** - Depth: **Arbitrates problem framing across teams** - spots when two areas are solving the same problem differently or optimizing conflicting outcomes, and reconciles the framing. Scope: multiple product areas.
- **P5** - Depth: **Defines the outcome architecture of the product org** - how team-level outcomes ladder to product-level results - and retires vanity outcomes that don't. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Frames the company's hardest, most ambiguous product problems** - the ones with no owner - into tractable outcome statements executives fund. Scope: company portfolio.

#### Jobs-to-be-done framing

*Anchor:* Christensen, Hall, Dillon & Duncan, *Competing Against Luck* (2016) - customers hire products to make progress in a circumstance; the job, not the demographic, predicts purchase. *Why:* JTBD gives problem definition a causal spine competitors' feature lists can't provide.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [proposed](../../contrib/2026-07-jobs-to-be-done-analysis.md) (PM-17 candidate) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **Restates a feature request as the job the customer is hiring it for**, including the circumstance and the progress sought, with coaching on the functional/emotional/social split. Scope: individual requests on one feature.
- **P2** - Depth: **Maps the main jobs in their area from interview evidence**, identifying what customers currently hire (including non-consumption and workarounds) and where those hires fail. Scope: their product area.
- **P3** - Depth: **Anchors their product's definition and boundaries in its core job**, using the job to rule scope in or out and to identify non-obvious competitors. Scope: their product; the job statement appears in the team's strategy doc, in their words.
- **P4** - Depth: **Aligns job maps across areas into one view of customer progress**, exposing where the portfolio makes customers stitch products together to finish one job. Scope: multiple product areas.
- **P5** - Depth: **Uses jobs analysis to shape org-level portfolio choices** - which jobs the org serves end-to-end, which it exits - in documents the leadership team debates from. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Frames company strategy in jobs terms** that reveal disruption threats and adjacent-market entries before the market makes them obvious. Scope: company portfolio.

### Assumption Testing & Validation

#### Assumption mapping & risk framing

*Anchor:* Bland & Osterwalder, *Testing Business Ideas* (2019); Cagan, *Inspired* (2nd ed., 2018) - desirability, viability, feasibility, and usability risks must be named and ranked before building. *Why:* the assumption map is the observable difference between validated bets and hope.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [proposed](../../contrib/2026-07-assumption-mapping-discovery-testing.md) (PM-18 candidate) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **Lists the assumptions behind their epic and sorts them by importance and evidence**, using the four-risks lens with coaching. Scope: one epic's risk map.
- **P2** - Depth: **Identifies the riskiest assumption before any build starts and says so in planning**, choosing what to test first rather than testing what is easiest. Scope: their area's bets.
- **P3** - Depth: **Surfaces the assumptions others treat as facts** - in their own bets and in reviews of others' - and kills initiatives whose load-bearing assumptions fail. Scope: a product's risk discipline; their pre-mortems are copied.
- **P4** - Depth: **Standardizes how multiple teams express and rank risk** so bets are comparable across areas, and challenges cross-team initiatives carrying untested compound assumptions. Scope: multiple product areas.
- **P5** - Depth: **Builds risk review into the org's investment process** - big funding asks arrive with assumption maps or go back - and calibrates the org's risk appetite explicitly. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Stress-tests portfolio bets' foundational assumptions in executive forums**, naming the belief that would have to be true and what evidence exists for it. Scope: company portfolio.

#### Assumption testing & experiments

*Anchor:* Ries, *The Lean Startup* (2011); Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits* (2021) - the smallest test that produces validated learning beats the most polished untested build. *Why:* experiment design skill is what makes "test before build" cheaper than building.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [proposed](../../contrib/2026-07-assumption-mapping-discovery-testing.md) (PM-18 candidate) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **Builds simple tests - fake-door links, clickable prototypes, concierge runs - from a design someone else specified**, and logs what was learned. Scope: one assumption at a time.
- **P2** - Depth: **Chooses the test type that matches the assumption** - prototype for usability, painted door for demand, Wizard of Oz for feasibility - and defines pass/fail before launching it. Scope: their area's assumption tests.
- **P3** - Depth: **Designs test sequences that retire the most risk per week of effort**, and rejects tests whose result couldn't change the decision. Scope: a product; their test designs are the examples in onboarding.
- **P4** - Depth: **Unblocks teams whose experiments keep coming back inconclusive**, redesigning the test rather than rerunning it bigger. Scope: multiple product areas.
- **P5** - Depth: **Builds the org's testing toolkit** - reusable painted-door infrastructure, prototype panels, templates - dropping the cost of a first test to hours. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Designs validation for bets too big to A/B** - new markets, pricing models, platforms - using staged commitments and falsifiable milestones. Scope: company portfolio.

#### Prototype & solution testing

*Anchor:* Olsen, *The Lean Product Playbook* (2015) - iterating prototypes through user tests against the product-market-fit pyramid; Cagan, *Inspired* (2nd ed., 2018) - prototypes are for learning, products are for earning. *Why:* testing solutions before building them is the core mechanic that separates discovery from opinion.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [proposed](../../contrib/2026-07-assumption-mapping-discovery-testing.md) (PM-18 candidate) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **Runs moderated tests of an existing prototype from a task script**, noting where users struggle without explaining the design to them. Scope: usability of one feature.
- **P2** - Depth: **Pairs with design to build the right-fidelity prototype for the question at hand** and iterates it between test rounds, reporting what changed and why. Scope: solution tests across their product area.
- **P3** - Depth: **Chooses the testing method by risk** - prototype, Wizard-of-Oz, live-data test - and calls when the evidence is sufficient to commit to build. Scope: their product; design partners treat them as a peer in test planning.
- **P4** - Depth: **Reviews other teams' solution tests before major commitments** and catches tests that can only confirm, never falsify. Scope: multiple product areas.
- **P5** - Depth: **Builds the org's prototyping-and-testing muscle** - tooling, research-ops support, fidelity norms - so testing before building is the cheap default everywhere. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Represents test-before-build as company doctrine**, defending the practice when executive pressure pushes for committed roadmaps without evidence. Scope: company portfolio.

## Product Strategy & Vision

### Strategy & Positioning

#### Product strategy formulation

*Anchor:* Rumelt, *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* (2011) - real strategy is diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent action; Cagan, *Empowered* (2020) - product strategy as focused insight. *Why:* most "strategies" are goals; the craft is the diagnosis.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [PM-04](../../data/capabilities.md#pm-04) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **Explains their team's strategy in their own words** and connects their epic's work to it when asked. Scope: consumes strategy for one epic.
- **P2** - Depth: **Derives their area's priorities from the product strategy explicitly**, writing them down as choices - what they are doing and what they are explicitly not - and flags work that doesn't serve it. Scope: a product area.
- **P3** - Depth: **Writes the strategy for their product** - a diagnosis, a guiding policy, and the coherent bets - and defends the kill-list it implies in front of the stakeholders who asked. Scope: owns strategy for a product; the team quotes the strategy when declining work.
- **P4** - Depth: **Writes strategies other PMs adopt as the frame for their own areas**, and reviews area strategies for incoherence between diagnosis and bets. Scope: multiple product areas.
- **P5** - Depth: **Shapes the product org's strategy** - where to concentrate, what to starve - in documents leadership funds against. Scope: product organization.
- **P6** - Depth: **Co-authors company strategy from the product side**, and the portfolio's shape reflects arguments they made. Scope: company portfolio.

#### Positioning & differentiation

*Anchor:* Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* (2019); Moore, *Crossing the Chasm* (3rd ed., 2014) - deliberate positioning against the right competitive alternatives determines whether strengths read as value. *Why:* an unpositioned product is positioned by accident, usually badly.


| OCF | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | P6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [PM-04](../../data/capabilities.md#pm-04) | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **P1** - Depth: **States who the product is for, what it replaces, and why it wins in two sentences** consistent with the team's positioning canvas. Scope: describes their feature's value accurately.
- **P2** - Depth: **Maintains the positioning for their area** - target segment, competitive alternatives, differentiated value - and flags when a proposed feature blurs it. Scope: a product area's positioning artifacts.
- **P3** - Depth: **Repositions a product deliberately when the market shifts**, running the analysis of alternatives and trade-offs in writing; marketing builds messaging from their positioning work. Scope: a product; sales and marketing treat their positioning doc as source of truth.
- **P4** - Depth: **Keeps positioning coherent across a product family**, resolving where products in the portfolio overlap, undercut, or contradict each other in-market. Scope: multiple product areas.

#### A/B test design & analysis

*Anchor:* Kohavi, Tang & Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments* (2020) - controlled experiments as the gold standard for causal claims, and the catalog of ways they silently lie (SRM, peeking, novelty effects). *Why:* a PM who can't read an experiment honestly ships noise with confidence.

- **P5** - Depth: **Sets the org's launch discipline** - readiness standards, ramp policy, review practice - and is the escalation point for the launches with company-level blast radius. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Owns company-defining launches** - the ones with board visibility and market coverage - and sets the standard for what the company considers launch-ready. Scope: company portfolio.

#### Post-launch iteration & lifecycle

*Anchor:* Olsen, *The Lean Product Playbook* (2015) - the build-measure-learn loop continues after launch toward product-market fit; Perri, *Escaping the Build Trap* (2018) - shipping is the beginning of learning, not the end of the project. *Why:* most product value is created in the iterations after v1; teams that move on at launch strand it.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **P1** - Depth: **Tracks their feature's post-launch metrics against the targets set pre-launch** and reports the gap honestly, proposing the first follow-up fix. Scope: one feature's first iterations.
- **P2** - Depth: **Budgets iteration capacity into every launch plan** and runs the loop - measure, diagnose, fix, re-measure - until the feature hits its outcome or is deliberately parked. Scope: their product area.
- **P3** - Depth: **Makes the persevere/pivot/kill call on their product's bets** on the evidence rather than sunk cost, and sunsets features with a migration plan, resisting the portfolio-of-zombies default. Scope: their product; the deprecation they ran is the template others use.
- **P4** - Depth: **Audits post-launch follow-through across teams**, surfacing launched-and-abandoned features and getting iteration capacity re-protected. Scope: multiple product areas.
- **P5** - Depth: **Manages lifecycle at org level** - which products get investment, maintenance, or end-of-life - with criteria applied consistently enough that teams predict the outcome. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Makes the company's hardest lifecycle calls** - killing revenue-bearing products, betting through the J-curve - with reasoning the executive team adopts. Scope: company portfolio.

- **P5** - Depth: **Designs the org's PM hiring system** - competency-based loops, debrief norms, onboarding handoff - and the org's hiring quality is visibly their work. Scope: product org.
- **P6** - Depth: **Sets the company's bar for product talent** - the hiring philosophy and craft standards that shape who the company hires and attracts, cited by candidates as why they joined. Scope: company portfolio.
