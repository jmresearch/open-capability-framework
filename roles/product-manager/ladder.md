# Product Manager (IC Technical, P1-P6) - Canonical — Career Ladder

Canonical six-level IC ladder for product managers who own discovery-to-delivery outcomes for a product area or capability, consolidated by union from five independent sources; P3 Senior Product Manager is the terminal level.


| Level | Title | Scope | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Associate Product Manager | one feature/epic | Learns the craft; ships well-defined slices with guidance |
| P2 | Product Manager | a product area | Runs discovery-to-delivery independently for an area |
| P3 | Senior Product Manager | a product/capability (terminal level) | Owns outcomes for a product; sets the bar others copy |
| P4 | Staff/Group Product Manager | multiple product areas | Aligns strategy and craft across several product teams |
| P5 | Principal Product Manager | product organization | Sets product direction and practice the org builds against |
| P6 | Distinguished PM / Head of Product Craft | company portfolio | Shapes portfolio bets and the company's product craft |

## Product Discovery & User Research

#### Continuous Discovery

##### Customer interviewing

*Anchor:* Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021) — weekly customer touchpoints with story-based, non-leading questions are the foundational discovery habit; substituted for Portigal's Interviewing Users (no bibliography entry) with fitzpatrick2013-momtest, which names the exact leading-question failure mode a script and review exist to catch.

- **P1 (= P1)** — Runs scripted customer interviews and notes findings under a senior researcher's guidance. *Evidenced by:* Runs a scripted interview from a lead's guide, avoids the leading-question traps the Mom Test warns about, and writes up verbatim notes for the lead to review. *Scope:* one feature/epic. *Why this level:* PM-01 P1 ('Runs scripted customer interviews and notes findings under a senior researcher's guidance.') is the guided bar: running a prepared story-based interview and debriefing with a lead is Fitzpatrick's script-and-review discipline for a novice who has not yet earned unsupervised interview judgment. - **P2 (= P2)** — Plans and conducts standard discovery for a feature, synthesizing themes with normal review. *Evidenced by:* Keeps a weekly interview cadence going without being reminded, writes their own discussion guides, and turns each session into a snapshot teammates actually read. *Scope:* a product area. *Why this level:* PM-01 P2 ('Plans and conducts standard discovery for a feature, synthesizing themes with normal review.') matches an Associate PM's promotion to running discovery independently — Torres's weekly-cadence habit performed without redesigning the method each time, with normal review rather than a senior researcher's guidance. (Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test: How to Talk to Customers and Learn If Your Business Is a Good Idea When Everyone Is Lying to You (self-published, 2013); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **P3 (= P3)** — Independently frames ambiguous problems, selects fitting methods, and separates signal from noise. *Evidenced by:* Builds the recruiting pipeline for their product, brings engineers and designers into sessions, and redirects an interview that drifts into feature-request collection back to behavior. *Scope:* a product/capability (terminal level). *Why this level:* PM-01 P3 ('Independently frames ambiguous problems, selects fitting methods, and separates signal from noise.') is the capability-owning, terminal-level bar: owning the interviewing practice for a whole product — building the pipeline, pulling engineers into sessions, and redirecting drift — is Torres's continuous-discovery habit exercised with full autonomy, not just performed on schedule. (Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test: How to Talk to Customers and Learn If Your Business Is a Good Idea When Everyone Is Lying to You (self-published, 2013); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **P4 (= P4)** — Designs the team's discovery approach and untangles findings others find contradictory. *Evidenced by:* Reviews other PMs' discussion guides and sits in on their sessions, and the snapshot format spreading across teams traces back to their template. *Scope:* multiple product areas. *Why this level:* PM-01 P4 ('Designs the team's discovery approach and untangles findings others find contradictory.') is Staff-scope: interviewing technique that spreads to other teams' guides and snapshot formats is exactly Torres's discipline codified beyond one practitioner into something other PMs adopt. (Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test: How to Talk to Customers and Learn If Your Business Is a Good Idea When Everyone Is Lying to You (self-published, 2013); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **P5 (= P5)** — Sets the org's research strategy and anticipates emerging customer needs before they surface. *Evidenced by:* Stands up the org's recruiting panel, consent process, and interview-snapshot repository, and audits whether a team's customer contact is real or theater. *Scope:* product organization. *Why this level:* PM-01 P5 ('Sets the org's research strategy and anticipates emerging customer needs before they surface.') matches Principal-level authority: installing the org's interviewing infrastructure (recruiting panels, consent norms, snapshot repositories) is org-wide standard-setting beyond any one product's cadence. (Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test: How to Talk to Customers and Learn If Your Business Is a Good Idea When Everyone Is Lying to You (self-published, 2013); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **P6 (= P6)** — Defines what rigorous product discovery means and shapes the discipline's practice externally. *Evidenced by:* Sets the company's public standard for what counts as real customer contact and defends it in talks or writing that other practitioners cite. *Scope:* company portfolio. *Why this level:* PM-01 P6 ('Defines what rigorous product discovery means and shapes the discipline's practice externally.') is the company/external-authority register: setting the company's standard for customer contact and arguing it externally is the same register Torres's own book occupies as an externally cited discovery doctrine. (Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test: How to Talk to Customers and Learn If Your Business Is a Good Idea When Everyone Is Lying to You (self-published, 2013); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

(Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); Cagan, INSPIRED: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love, 2nd Edition (Wiley, 2018); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

(Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); Cagan, INSPIRED: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love, 2nd Edition (Wiley, 2018); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

#### Synthesis & Customer Modeling

##### Opportunity mapping

*Anchor:* Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021) — the opportunity solution tree keeps teams solving customer needs rather than shipping their first idea; self-sourced capability (PM-15 was minted in 2026-07 directly from this ladder's own prior consolidation prose — see scope_note) so the evidence below is written fresh, not carried over from the old ladder.md.

- **P1 (= P1)** — Places research findings onto an existing opportunity map correctly, distinguishing opportunities from solutions with coaching. *Evidenced by:* Adds a newly-interviewed customer's pain point to their lead's opportunity tree in the right branch, asking when unsure whether it names a need or a disguised feature request. *Scope:* one feature/epic. *Why this level:* PM-15 P1 ('Places research findings onto an existing opportunity map correctly, distinguishing opportunities from solutions with coaching.') is the guided bar: placing findings on a map someone else owns, with coaching to tell an opportunity from a solution, is Torres's foundational distinction taught before it is practiced independently. (Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **P2 (= P2)** — Builds and maintains the opportunity map for their area, sizing and pruning branches against evidence without prompting. *Evidenced by:* Keeps their product area's opportunity tree current between planning cycles, retiring branches no interview has touched in months and adding new ones as evidence accumulates. *Scope:* a product area. *Why this level:* PM-15 P2 ('Builds and maintains the opportunity map for their area, sizing and pruning branches against evidence without prompting.') matches independent operation: building and pruning the tree for one's own area without prompting is Torres's opportunity-solution-tree maintenance run as a standard, repeatable habit rather than a coached exercise. (Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **P3 (= P3)** — Structures messy, contested problem spaces into maps that make trade-offs explicit, and defends why one opportunity is pursued over its siblings. *Evidenced by:* Walks a skeptical stakeholder through why the team is pursuing one opportunity over three plausible siblings, pointing at the specific interview evidence behind the call. *Scope:* a product/capability (terminal level). *Why this level:* PM-15 P3 ('Structures messy, contested problem spaces into maps that make trade-offs explicit, and defends why one opportunity is pursued over its siblings.') is the terminal, capability-owning bar: structuring a contested problem space and defending why one branch is pursued over its siblings is Torres's own description of tree-pruning as a judgment call, not a mechanical sizing exercise. (Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **P4 (= P4)** — Reconciles overlapping opportunity maps across teams, exposing duplicate bets and orphaned opportunities no single team could see. *Evidenced by:* Merges two teams' opportunity trees ahead of a joint planning cycle and surfaces a customer need both teams were separately half-solving without knowing about the other's work. *Scope:* multiple product areas. *Why this level:* PM-15 P4 ('Reconciles overlapping opportunity maps across teams, exposing duplicate bets and orphaned opportunities no single team could see.') is Staff-scope: reconciling overlapping trees across teams and exposing duplicate or orphaned opportunities is one person's mapping method applied across multiple teams' trees — the cross-team synthesis Torres reserves for senior practice. (Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **P5 (= P5)** — Makes opportunity mapping an organization's default synthesis method; investment reviews open from the mapped opportunity space. *Evidenced by:* Gets a quarterly investment review to open with the mapped opportunity space instead of a list of proposed features, and rejects a funding request that skips the map. *Scope:* product organization. *Why this level:* PM-15 P5 ('Makes opportunity mapping an organization's default synthesis method; investment reviews open from the mapped opportunity space.') matches Principal authority: making opportunity mapping the org's default synthesis method, so investment reviews open from the mapped space, is organization-wide adoption of the method, not one team's artifact. (Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **P6 (= P6)** — Shapes how organizations represent customer opportunity at portfolio level; their framing methods are cited and reused beyond their own organization. *Evidenced by:* Publishes the opportunity-mapping method used across the company's product lines and fields questions about it from PMs at other companies trying to adopt it. *Scope:* company portfolio. *Why this level:* PM-15 P6 ('Shapes how organizations represent customer opportunity at portfolio level; their framing methods are cited and reused beyond their own organization.') is company/portfolio, external-authority scope: shaping how organizations represent customer opportunity at portfolio level, with methods reused beyond one's own organization, is the register Torres's own tree occupies externally. (Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (Product Talk LLC, 2021); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

- **P3 (= P3)** — Sets technical direction for a domain; others align to their calls. *Evidenced by:* Sets the discovery and spec-writing bar their product's team aligns to without being told, handling the hardest ambiguous calls unsupervised. *Scope:* a product/capability (terminal level). *Why this level:* LI-03 P3 ('Sets technical direction for a domain; others align to their calls.') is the terminal, capability-owning bar: setting the craft direction for a domain that others align to is Larson's 'Solver' archetype operating with full autonomy over ambiguous problems — the local go-to, handling hard cases unsupervised. (Cagan & Jones, EMPOWERED: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products (Wiley, 2020); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

(Cagan & Jones, EMPOWERED: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products (Wiley, 2020); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

(Cagan & Jones, EMPOWERED: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products (Wiley, 2020); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

*Anchor:* Cagan & Jones, EMPOWERED: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products (Wiley, 2020) — building the team through hiring and placement is the product leader's compounding lever — a strong PM organization is built one calibrated hire at a time, not backfilled after the fact.

- **P4 (= P4)** — Drives hiring strategy across teams; builds the interviewing bar. *Evidenced by:* Builds the PM interviewing bar three other teams now hire against, driving hiring strategy across the group. *Scope:* multiple product areas. *Why this level:* LI-02 P4 ('Drives hiring strategy across teams; builds the interviewing bar.') is Staff-scope: driving hiring strategy across teams and building the interviewing bar is the org-wide analog of one team's structured-interviewing guide, scaled to multiple teams. (Cagan & Jones, EMPOWERED: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products (Wiley, 2020); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **P5 (= P5)** — Sets the org's talent strategy. *Evidenced by:* Sets the product org's talent strategy — leveling, sourcing priorities, workforce plan — for the year. *Scope:* product organization. *Why this level:* LI-02 P5 ('Sets the org's talent strategy.') matches Principal authority: setting the org's talent strategy — workforce planning, leveling, sourcing priorities — is anticipating direction and setting standards rather than executing within them. (Cagan & Jones, EMPOWERED: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products (Wiley, 2020); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **P6 (= P6)** — Shapes company-wide talent philosophy. *Evidenced by:* Shapes the company's talent philosophy for product roles in a way that outlasts their own tenure in the org. *Scope:* company portfolio. *Why this level:* LI-02 P6 ('Shapes company-wide talent philosophy.') is company/portfolio scope: shaping company-wide talent philosophy is a talent philosophy that shapes how an entire company thinks about hiring PMs, outlasting any one leader's tenure. (Cagan & Jones, EMPOWERED: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products (Wiley, 2020); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
