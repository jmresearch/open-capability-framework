# Platform Engineering Manager (M1-M6) - Canonical — Career Ladder

People-leadership ladder (M1-M6) for internal developer-platform, DevEx, and infrastructure teams, carrying the full engineering-management load plus platform-as-a-product, reliability, paved-road, and self-service accountabilities.


| Level | Title | Scope | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Platform Engineering Manager | one platform team | builds one healthy platform team and the platform-as-a-product fundamentals |
| M2 | Senior Platform Engineering Manager | a complex / critical platform team | runs a high-stakes platform team and its reliability and DevEx outcomes |
| M3 | Director of Platform Engineering | a platform domain (cross-team) | sets strategy and paved-road standards for a cross-team platform domain |
| M4 | Senior Director of Platform Engineering | a platform sub-org | builds the operating system and architecture a platform sub-org runs on |
| M5 | VP of Platform Engineering | the platform function | sets platform-function direction and represents developer productivity to the business |
| M6 | SVP / Head of Platform | org-wide platform | sets org-wide platform vision, operating model, and the company-level infrastructure bets |

(Tuckman, Developmental Sequence in Small Groups, Psychological Bulletin 63(6), 384-399 (1965); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M5 (= P5)** — Designs how an organization absorbs step-change growth — acquisitions, new sites, doubled headcount — without culture dilution; integration plans carry named cultural mechanisms. *Evidenced by:* Sets the norms that keep newly formed platform teams productive through function-wide scaling. *Scope:* the platform function. *Why this level:* EM-13 P5 ('designs how an organization absorbs step-change growth...without culture dilution') matches VP-level ownership of function-wide growth events -- acquisitions, new sites -- beyond any one team's formation. (Tuckman, Developmental Sequence in Small Groups, Psychological Bulletin 63(6), 384-399 (1965); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M6 (= P6)** — Makes team formation an organization-wide capability — new groups spin up predictably without heroics — and audits that the machinery still works. *Evidenced by:* Pioneers the team-formation playbook other engineering functions reuse. *Scope:* org-wide platform. *Why this level:* EM-13 P6 ('makes team formation an organization-wide capability...audits that the machinery still works') is the SVP register: forming teams is now infrastructure, not an event Tuckman's model describes per-team. (Tuckman, Developmental Sequence in Small Groups, Psychological Bulletin 63(6), 384-399 (1965); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))

- **M1 (= P2)** — Stays effective and steady under pressure — recovers from setbacks, manages their own energy, and models a sustainable pace others learn from watching. *Evidenced by:* Logs off after a brutal on-call week and says so openly instead of powering through, and the team notices and starts doing the same. *Scope:* one platform team. *Why this level:* LI-10 P2 ('stays effective and steady under pressure -- recovers from setbacks, manages their own energy, and models a sustainable pace others learn from watching') is Loehr & Schwartz's managed-energy discipline at first-line scope. (Loehr & Schwartz, The Power of Full Engagement: Managing Energy, Not Time, Is the Key to High Performance and Personal Renewal (Free Press, 2003); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M2 (= P3)** — Holds up through prolonged pressure — incident sieges, crunch, organizational turbulence — helping the team stay resilient and refusing burnout-driven decisions. *Evidenced by:* Absorbs the stress of a severe outage period and keeps the team functioning rather than fraying. *Scope:* a complex / critical platform team. *Why this level:* LI-10 P3 ('holds up through prolonged pressure -- incident sieges, crunch, organizational turbulence -- helping the team stay resilient and refusing burnout-driven decisions') is sustained composure through a critical team's real operational pressure. (Loehr & Schwartz, The Power of Full Engagement: Managing Energy, Not Time, Is the Key to High Performance and Personal Renewal (Free Press, 2003); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M3 (= P4)** — Builds resilience into how multiple teams operate — on-call health, recovery after pushes, load balancing — and repairs burnout risk at scale. *Evidenced by:* Acts on a burnout signal across the domain before it becomes attrition, changing the load rather than the people. *Scope:* a platform domain (cross-team). *Why this level:* LI-10 P4 ('builds resilience into how multiple teams operate -- on-call health, recovery after pushes, load balancing -- and repairs burnout risk at scale') is the sustainable-pace norm other leads adopt -- Director-level. (Loehr & Schwartz, The Power of Full Engagement: Managing Energy, Not Time, Is the Key to High Performance and Personal Renewal (Free Press, 2003); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M4 (= P4)** — Builds resilience into how multiple teams operate — on-call health, recovery after pushes, load balancing — and repairs burnout risk at scale. *Evidenced by:* Reshapes on-call and workload across the sub-org so resilience does not depend on individual heroics. *Scope:* a platform sub-org. *Why this level:* M4 keeps P4; Senior Director scope is the operating norms a sub-org uses to keep pace sustainable through scaling and incidents, not a deeper resilience behavior. (Loehr & Schwartz, The Power of Full Engagement: Managing Energy, Not Time, Is the Key to High Performance and Personal Renewal (Free Press, 2003); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M5 (= P5)** — Stewards organizational resilience — capacity buffers, crisis rotation, energy management for senior leaders — and holds the line when business pressure argues for permanent crunch. *Evidenced by:* Sets the sustainable-pace expectations leaders across the function are held to. *Scope:* the platform function. *Why this level:* LI-10 P5 ('stewards organizational resilience -- capacity buffers, crisis rotation, energy management for senior leaders -- and holds the line when business pressure argues for permanent crunch') matches VP-level ownership. (Loehr & Schwartz, The Power of Full Engagement: Managing Energy, Not Time, Is the Key to High Performance and Personal Renewal (Free Press, 2003); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M6 (= P6)** — Shapes a resilient, sustainable culture across an organization; the organization absorbs shocks without breaking people, and the practice is referenced beyond it. *Evidenced by:* Pioneers a sustainable-operations model peer functions adopt. *Scope:* org-wide platform. *Why this level:* LI-10 P6 ('shapes a resilient, sustainable culture across an organization; the organization absorbs shocks without breaking people') is Loehr & Schwartz's energy-management thesis applied at the org-wide register. (Loehr & Schwartz, The Power of Full Engagement: Managing Energy, Not Time, Is the Key to High Performance and Personal Renewal (Free Press, 2003); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))

#### Judgment & Integrity

##### Decision-making under uncertainty

*Anchor:* Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011) — sound decisions require guarding against bias and calibrating confidence to the evidence

| OCF | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 | M6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [LI-04](../../data/capabilities.md#li-04) — Decision-Making under Uncertainty | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **M1 (= P2)** — Decides independently within their components; evaluates alternatives honestly. *Evidenced by:* Makes a reversible platform decision quickly rather than stalling for certainty that will not come. *Scope:* one platform team. *Why this level:* LI-04 P2 ('decides independently within their components; evaluates alternatives honestly') is making timely calls with incomplete information and stating assumptions -- Kahneman's System 2 check applied at first-line scope. (Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M2 (= P3)** — Makes architectural choices for a domain that hold up over months. *Evidenced by:* Frames a one-way-door platform decision explicitly and gathers the evidence its weight warrants. *Scope:* a complex / critical platform team. *Why this level:* LI-04 P3 ('makes architectural choices for a domain that hold up over months') is making high-stakes, hard-to-reverse calls on a critical platform and guarding against their own bias, unaided. (Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M3 (= P4)** — Foresees second-order consequences; sets decision frameworks for teams. *Evidenced by:* Installs a decision framework several teams now use to make calls under ambiguity. *Scope:* a platform domain (cross-team). *Why this level:* LI-04 P4 ('foresees second-order consequences; sets decision frameworks for teams') is the decision-making discipline other leads adopt, with reversibility tests and recorded rationale -- Director-level. (Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M4 (= P4)** — Foresees second-order consequences; sets decision frameworks for teams. *Evidenced by:* Raises the quality of consequential decisions across the sub-org by making the reasoning and the owner explicit. *Scope:* a platform sub-org. *Why this level:* M4 keeps P4; Senior Director scope is the decision-rights and judgment norms a sub-org runs on, not a deeper decision behavior. (Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M5 (= P5)** — Sets decision-making frameworks for the org. *Evidenced by:* Sets the decision standard leaders across the function are calibrated to. *Scope:* the platform function. *Why this level:* LI-04 P5 ('sets decision-making frameworks for the org') matches VP-level ownership of how the function makes irreversible, high-ambiguity bets. (Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M6 (= P6)** — Visionary judgment; sees structural causes others miss. *Evidenced by:* Pioneers a decision model peer functions adopt. *Scope:* org-wide platform. *Why this level:* LI-04 P6 ('visionary judgment; sees structural causes others miss') is Kahneman's bias-guarded reasoning applied at the org-wide, structural-insight register. (Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))

##### Ethics, integrity & trust

*Anchor:* Mayer, Davis & Schoorman, An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust, Academy of Management Review 20(3), 709-734 (1995) — trust is built on ability, benevolence, and integrity, and platform leaders hold outsized access and impact

| OCF | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 | M6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [LI-11](../../data/capabilities.md#li-11) — Integrity & Trust | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **M1 (= P3)** — Makes the unpopular-but-right call under pressure and absorbs the cost personally; protects the person who raised the flag and handles sensitive information impeccably. *Evidenced by:* Makes the honest call on a platform reliability or security risk even when it is inconvenient to admit. *Scope:* one platform team. *Why this level:* LI-11 P3 ('makes the unpopular-but-right call under pressure and absorbs the cost personally; protects the person who raised the flag') is Mayer, Davis & Schoorman's integrity dimension of trust under real stakes -- the catalog places this at the first management level given platform's outsized access to others' systems and data, above P2's personal consistency. (Mayer, Davis & Schoorman, An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust, Academy of Management Review 20(3), 709-734 (1995); ACM, ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018))
- **M2 (= P3)** — Makes the unpopular-but-right call under pressure and absorbs the cost personally; protects the person who raised the flag and handles sensitive information impeccably. *Evidenced by:* Refuses a shortcut that would trade user trust or safety for speed, and says why. *Scope:* a complex / critical platform team. *Why this level:* M2 holds the same P3 bar; the Senior EM differentiator is holding the ethical line where access, data, and reliability carry real stakes on a critical platform, not a deeper integrity behavior. (Mayer, Davis & Schoorman, An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust, Academy of Management Review 20(3), 709-734 (1995); ACM, ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018))
- **M3 (= P4)** — Serves as the honest broker across teams — disputing parties accept their account of the facts — and carries uncomfortable systemic truths to the room that can fix them. *Evidenced by:* Establishes the do-the-right-thing expectations several teams now hold each other to. *Scope:* a platform domain (cross-team). *Why this level:* LI-11 P4 ('serves as the honest broker across teams -- disputing parties accept their account of the facts -- and carries uncomfortable systemic truths to the room that can fix them') is the ethical norm other leads adopt around access, data, and honest disclosure -- Director-level. (Mayer, Davis & Schoorman, An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust, Academy of Management Review 20(3), 709-734 (1995); ACM, ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018))
- **M4 (= P4)** — Serves as the honest broker across teams — disputing parties accept their account of the facts — and carries uncomfortable systemic truths to the room that can fix them. *Evidenced by:* Makes candid, blameless honesty the norm across the sub-org even when the news is bad. *Scope:* a platform sub-org. *Why this level:* M4 keeps P4; Senior Director scope is the trust and integrity expectations a sub-org operates by, from privileged-access ethics to honest reporting, not a deeper integrity behavior. (Mayer, Davis & Schoorman, An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust, Academy of Management Review 20(3), 709-734 (1995); ACM, ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018))
- **M5 (= P5)** — Builds mechanisms that make integrity cheap at scale — ethics review in design, safe escalation with follow-through, fairness systems whose losers affirm the process — and stops launches on ethical grounds with the business case for trust. *Evidenced by:* Sets the integrity standard leaders across the function are trusted to uphold. *Scope:* the platform function. *Why this level:* LI-11 P5 ('builds mechanisms that make integrity cheap at scale -- ethics review in design, safe escalation with follow-through...stops launches on ethical grounds') matches VP-level ownership of the function's ethical posture on its outsized access and impact. (Mayer, Davis & Schoorman, An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust, Academy of Management Review 20(3), 709-734 (1995); ACM, ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018))
- **M6 (= P6)** — Holds an organization's line when the profitable path and the right path diverge — the hard disclosure, the walked-away revenue — and those moments become the story the culture tells about itself. *Evidenced by:* Pioneers an ethics model peer functions and the wider field reference. *Scope:* org-wide platform. *Why this level:* LI-11 P6 ('holds an organization's line when the profitable path and the right path diverge -- the hard disclosure, the walked-away revenue') is the ACM Code of Ethics's public-good primacy applied at the org-defining register. (Mayer, Davis & Schoorman, An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust, Academy of Management Review 20(3), 709-734 (1995); ACM, ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018))
