# AI Engineer (IC Technical, E1-E6) - Canonical — Career Ladder

Canonical six-level IC ladder for engineers building LLM-powered and agentic systems, consolidated by union from six independent same-methodology sources; E3 Senior is the terminal level.


| Level | Title | Scope | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Associate AI Engineer | task | Learns the AI stack and ships well-scoped tasks with guidance |
| E2 | AI Engineer | component | Owns model-backed components end to end, independently |
| E3 | Senior AI Engineer | capability / domain (terminal level) | Owns an AI capability; the quality bar for a domain; terminal level |
| E4 | Staff AI Engineer | multiple teams | Sets patterns and standards multiple teams build against |
| E5 | Senior Staff AI Engineer | organization | Drives org-wide AI engineering strategy |
| E6 | Principal AI Engineer | company | Sets company-level technical direction for AI |

## Software Engineering Craft

#### Design & Architecture

##### System design & architecture

*Anchor:* Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications: The Big Ideas Behind Reliable, Scalable, and Maintainable Systems (O'Reilly, 2017) — AI features live inside distributed systems; the data-flow and consistency trade-offs Kleppmann catalogs are the load-bearing design decisions, model calls included.

| OCF | E1 | E2 | E3 | E4 | E5 | E6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [SWE-05](../../data/capabilities.md#swe-05) — Software Design & Architecture | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **E1 (= P1)** — Implements designs specified by others; asks why, not just how. *Evidenced by:* Explains the system's architecture in their own words. *Scope:* task. *Why this level:* SWE-05 P1 ('Implements designs specified by others; asks why, not just how.') is the guided bar an Associate operates at — implementing a design someone else made and asking about an unclear boundary is exactly the novice-to-competent register, applied to where model calls sit in the request path. (Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications: The Big Ideas Behind Reliable, Scalable, and Maintainable Systems (O'Reilly, 2017); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E2 (= P2)** — Designs sound components; understands coupling, cohesion, interface design. *Evidenced by:* Designs a component and writes the short design note for it. *Scope:* component. *Why this level:* SWE-05 P2 ('Designs sound components; understands coupling, cohesion, interface design.') is the independent-component bar an AI Engineer operates at — designing one sound component — isolating model calls behind clear interfaces — is independent practice at component scope, not yet a capability's whole design. (Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications: The Big Ideas Behind Reliable, Scalable, and Maintainable Systems (O'Reilly, 2017); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E3 (= P3)** — Designs systems for evolution: clean seams, versioned interfaces, replaceable parts. *Evidenced by:* Selects patterns by trade-off in design docs that name the alternatives and failure modes. *Scope:* capability / domain (terminal level). *Why this level:* SWE-05 P3 ('Designs systems for evolution: clean seams, versioned interfaces, replaceable parts.') is the domain-owning bar for a Senior — designing for evolution (swappable models, versioned prompts, isolated nondeterminism) across a capability is 'clean seams, replaceable parts' one level past a single component. E3 is this ladder's terminal level (scope_note): sustained P3 mastery here is a complete career, not a waypoint. (Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications: The Big Ideas Behind Reliable, Scalable, and Maintainable Systems (O'Reilly, 2017); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E4 (= P4)** — Mastery of architecture for complex systems; sets patterns and prevents drift. *Evidenced by:* Writes the reference architectures other teams instantiate. *Scope:* multiple teams. *Why this level:* SWE-05 P4 ('Mastery of architecture for complex systems; sets patterns and prevents drift.') is Staff-scope — writing reference architectures other teams instantiate is pattern-setting read across multiple teams, not one capability's design reviews. (Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications: The Big Ideas Behind Reliable, Scalable, and Maintainable Systems (O'Reilly, 2017); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **E5 (= P5)** — Sets multi-year architecture direction across the org. *Evidenced by:* Sets the architectural direction for AI systems across the org. *Scope:* organization. *Why this level:* SWE-05 P5 ('Sets multi-year architecture direction across the org.') matches Senior Staff's org-wide authority — setting AI architectural direction across the org and retiring dead-end patterns is the multi-year, org-scope direction P5 names. (Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications: The Big Ideas Behind Reliable, Scalable, and Maintainable Systems (O'Reilly, 2017); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **E6 (= P6)** — Authoritative and externally credible; defines architectural approach others follow. *Evidenced by:* Defines the company's long-horizon technical bets for AI systems. *Scope:* company. *Why this level:* SWE-05 P6 ('Authoritative and externally credible; defines architectural approach others follow.') is the company/industry-scope bar — defining the company's build-vs-buy and platform-vs-product bets in writing executives act on is what external credibility cashes out to at company scope. (Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications: The Big Ideas Behind Reliable, Scalable, and Maintainable Systems (O'Reilly, 2017); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

##### API & service design

*Anchor:* Fielding, Architectural Styles and the Design of Network-based Software Architectures (PhD dissertation, University of California Irvine, 2000) — Bloch's original API-design essay has no bibliography entry; Fielding's REST dissertation is the foundational, catalog-sourced text SWE-07's own P1/P2 bars already cite for interface design, and AI features leak nondeterminism to consumers unless the contract deliberately contains it.

- **E1 (= P1)** — Understands HTTP/REST and the deploy pipeline; makes guided changes. *Evidenced by:* Implements endpoints to an existing API contract, including error responses, and flags mismatches between spec and behavior. *Scope:* task. *Why this level:* SWE-07 P1 ('Understands HTTP/REST and the deploy pipeline; makes guided changes.') is the guided bar an Associate operates at — implementing endpoints to an existing contract, including error responses, is the guided register — the contract already exists, they fill it in. (Fielding, Architectural Styles and the Design of Network-based Software Architectures (PhD dissertation, University of California Irvine, 2000); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E2 (= P2)** — Designs clean APIs; understands rate limits, retries, timeouts, idempotency. *Evidenced by:* Designs the API surface for a component and documents it well enough that a consumer needs no walkthrough. *Scope:* component. *Why this level:* SWE-07 P2 ('Designs clean APIs; understands rate limits, retries, timeouts, idempotency.') is the independent-component bar an AI Engineer operates at — designing clean APIs with rate limits, retries, timeouts and idempotency for one component is independent interface design at component scope. - **E3 (= P3)** — Designs services for scale and cost; understands distributed failure modes. *Evidenced by:* Designs contracts that isolate consumers from model and provider changes and rejects designs that leak provider details to callers. *Scope:* capability / domain (terminal level). *Why this level:* SWE-07 P3 ('Designs services for scale and cost; understands distributed failure modes.') is the domain-owning bar for a Senior — designing services for scale and distributed failure modes — streaming, partial failure, nondeterminism handled explicitly — is what isolating consumers from model and provider changes requires for a whole capability. (Fielding, Architectural Styles and the Design of Network-based Software Architectures (PhD dissertation, University of California Irvine, 2000); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E4 (= P4)** — Expert in platform/service design (multi-tenancy, capacity, shared services). *Evidenced by:* Sets the API conventions multiple teams follow for model-backed services and retires inconsistent legacy surfaces with migration paths. *Scope:* multiple teams. *Why this level:* SWE-07 P4 ('Expert in platform/service design (multi-tenancy, capacity, shared services).') is Staff-scope — expert platform/service design across multi-tenancy and shared services is setting API conventions multiple teams follow, not one capability's contracts. (Fielding, Architectural Styles and the Design of Network-based Software Architectures (PhD dissertation, University of California Irvine, 2000); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **E5 (= P5)** — Sets interface standards used across the org. *Evidenced by:* Drives the org's interface strategy — which capabilities become shared platforms, and the deprecation policy that keeps the surface coherent. *Scope:* organization. *Why this level:* SWE-07 P5 ('Sets interface standards used across the org.') matches Senior Staff's org-wide authority — setting interface standards used across the org is the org-wide deprecation and interface-strategy authority P5 names. (Fielding, Architectural Styles and the Design of Network-based Software Architectures (PhD dissertation, University of California Irvine, 2000); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **E6 (= P6)** — Defines company-wide API approach; trusted with irreversible interface bets. *Evidenced by:* Shapes the company's external and partner-facing AI interfaces, balancing product commitments against provider volatility. *Scope:* company. *Why this level:* SWE-07 P6 ('Defines company-wide API approach; trusted with irreversible interface bets.') is the company/industry-scope bar — defining company-wide API approach and owning irreversible interface bets is the external, partner-facing register. (Fielding, Architectural Styles and the Design of Network-based Software Architectures (PhD dissertation, University of California Irvine, 2000); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

#### Code Craft & Implementation

##### Coding & implementation

*Anchor:* McConnell, Code Complete: A Practical Handbook of Software Construction, 2nd Edition (Microsoft Press, 2004) — AI code is still code; McConnell's implementation discipline is the base layer the rest of the ladder stands on.

| OCF | E1 | E2 | E3 | E4 | E5 | E6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [SWE-01](../../data/capabilities.md#swe-01) — Writing Code | [P1](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p1) | [P2](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p2) | [P3](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p3) | [P4](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p4) | [P5](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p5) | [P6](../../data/proficiency_scale.md#p6) |

- **E1 (= P1)** — Writes working, readable code in the team's language; debugs own changes. *Evidenced by:* Implements the retry-with-backoff helper for a flaky provider call exactly to spec and finds the off-by-one in their own token-counting logic before it reaches review. *Scope:* task. *Why this level:* SWE-01 P1 ('Writes working, readable code in the team's language; debugs own changes.') is the guided bar an Associate operates at — writing working, readable code and debugging their own changes is the novice-to-competent bar Code Complete's construction discipline describes. (McConnell, Code Complete: A Practical Handbook of Software Construction, 2nd Edition (Microsoft Press, 2004); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E2 (= P2)** — Proficient in the primary language/ecosystem; handles errors, async and concurrency basics correctly. *Evidenced by:* Ships the async job that reprocesses failed embedding batches, handling partial-batch failures and concurrent writes to the vector store without a teammate having to point them out. *Scope:* component. *Why this level:* SWE-01 P2 ('Proficient in the primary language/ecosystem; handles errors, async and concurrency basics correctly.') is the independent-component bar an AI Engineer operates at — handling errors, async patterns and concurrency basics correctly in idiomatic code independently is proficiency in the primary ecosystem at component scope. (McConnell, Code Complete: A Practical Handbook of Software Construction, 2nd Edition (Microsoft Press, 2004); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E3 (= P3)** — Code is a reference others learn from; debugs the hard problems (races, memory, nondeterminism). *Evidenced by:* Writes the concurrent request-batching code newer engineers copy verbatim, and is the one who finds the race condition in the streaming-response handler that only shows up under load. *Scope:* capability / domain (terminal level). *Why this level:* SWE-01 P3 ('Code is a reference others learn from; debugs the hard problems (races, memory, nondeterminism).') is the domain-owning bar for a Senior — code others use as the reference for the stack, and debugging race conditions and nondeterministic failures, is the 'reference others learn from' register applied to a capability. E3 is this ladder's terminal level (scope_note): sustained P3 mastery here is a complete career, not a waypoint. (McConnell, Code Complete: A Practical Handbook of Software Construction, 2nd Edition (Microsoft Press, 2004); SFIA Foundation, Skills Framework for the Information Age, version 9 (2024))
- **E4 (= P4)** — Mastery across multiple stacks/paradigms; solves problems others can't; sets implementation patterns. *Evidenced by:* Rewrites the team's Python retrieval service in Rust for a client with different concurrency guarantees, solving a memory-pressure bug in the vector index that stumped two other engineers. *Scope:* multiple teams. *Why this level:* SWE-01 P4 ('Mastery across multiple stacks/paradigms; solves problems others can't; sets implementation patterns.') is Staff-scope — solving implementation problems other teams can't, across multiple stacks, and landing shared libraries is mastery read across multiple teams rather than one capability. (McConnell, Code Complete: A Practical Handbook of Software Construction, 2nd Edition (Microsoft Press, 2004); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **E5 (= P5)** — Sets implementation standards adopted across the org; anticipates where code breaks at scale. *Evidenced by:* Sets the org's async-error-handling convention after a postmortem shows three teams' AI services failed the same way under provider timeout spikes. *Scope:* organization. *Why this level:* SWE-01 P5 ('Sets implementation standards adopted across the org; anticipates where code breaks at scale.') matches Senior Staff's org-wide authority — anticipating where implementations break at scale and shaping org-wide practice through load-bearing code is the org-scope standard-setting P5 names. (McConnell, Code Complete: A Practical Handbook of Software Construction, 2nd Edition (Microsoft Press, 2004); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))
- **E6 (= P6)** — Industry-level depth; authors foundational primitives and idioms others build on. *Evidenced by:* Writes the request-batching primitive now vendored into every AI service's client library company-wide, the reference implementation new hires study. *Scope:* company. *Why this level:* SWE-01 P6 ('Industry-level depth; authors foundational primitives and idioms others build on.') is the company/industry-scope bar — authoring foundational primitives used company-wide is the industry-level depth P6 describes. (McConnell, Code Complete: A Practical Handbook of Software Construction, 2nd Edition (Microsoft Press, 2004); Larson, Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (self-published, 2021))

#### Testing & AI-Assisted Development
