# Engineering Management (M1-M6) - Canonical — Career Ladder

People-leadership ladder (M1-M6) for engineering management, from first-line manager through SVP / org lead, spanning leadership & direction, execution & operations, people & talent, technical stewardship, communication & influence, and self & personal effectiveness.


| Level | Title | Scope | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Engineering Manager | one team | builds one healthy |
| M2 | Senior Engineering Manager | a complex / critical team | runs a high-stakes team and multiplies senior ICs |
| M3 | Director | a domain (cross-team) | aligns a cross-team domain through leaders and mechanisms |
| M4 | Senior Director | a sub-org | builds the operating system a sub-org runs on |
| M5 | VP | a function | sets functional direction and represents engineering to the business |
| M6 | SVP / Org Lead | org-wide | sets org-wide direction |

## Leadership & Direction

#### Strategy & Vision

##### Strategy Formulation

*Anchor:* Rumelt, Good Strategy Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Crown Business, 2011) — real strategy is a diagnosis plus a coherent guiding policy and actions, not goals restated as ambition

- **M1 (= P2)** — Develops components of strategy and standard analyses independently with normal review. *Evidenced by:* Writes a team charter that names the problem the team exists to solve, not just its goals. *Scope:* one team. *Why this level:* STRAT-01 P2 ('develops components of strategy and standard analyses independently with normal review') is Rumelt's diagnosis-plus-guiding-policy kernel applied to one team's plan, not yet the unaided strategic framing P3 expects for ambiguous problems. (Rumelt, Good Strategy Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Crown Business, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M2 (= P3)** — Frames ambiguous strategic problems and shapes coherent strategy autonomously; the go-to strategist. *Evidenced by:* Reframes a team's stalled 'strategy' around its actual obstacle after spotting it was really just goals restated as ambition. *Scope:* a complex / critical team. *Why this level:* STRAT-01 P3 ('frames ambiguous strategic problems and shapes coherent strategy autonomously; the go-to strategist') is Rumelt's kernel test -- catching a 'strategy' that is really goals restated as ambition -- applied unaided on a complex/critical team, the harder judgment call P2's component-level analysis does not require. (Rumelt, Good Strategy Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Crown Business, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M3 (= P4)** — Defines strategy-formulation approaches others adopt; resolves the hardest strategic trade-offs. *Evidenced by:* Publishes the domain strategy several teams plan against, naming the bets, non-goals, and rejected alternatives. *Scope:* a domain (cross-team). *Why this level:* STRAT-01 P4 ('defines strategy-formulation approaches others adopt; resolves the hardest strategic trade-offs') is a domain strategy several teams plan against, not one team's document -- Director-level per GitLab's cross-team scope. (Rumelt, Good Strategy Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Crown Business, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M4 (= P4)** — Defines strategy-formulation approaches others adopt; resolves the hardest strategic trade-offs. *Evidenced by:* Reconciles competing team strategies into one coherent investment thesis, cutting real programs to fund the guiding policy. *Scope:* a sub-org. *Why this level:* M4 keeps P4; a sub-org strategy reconciling competing team strategies into one coherent investment thesis is still the pattern-setting behavior STRAT-01 P4 describes, not yet P5's enterprise-wide direction-setting -- sub-org scope does not reach the enterprise register P5 requires, so this competency stays on the ladder's usual M4:P4 plateau (this repoints the pre-retrofit M4:P5 curve; platform-engineering-manager's R3 retrofit already flagged the same P5-at-sub-org miscalibration for its own STRAT-01 mapping). (Rumelt, Good Strategy Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Crown Business, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M5 (= P5)** — Sets the enterprise strategic direction; anticipates major shifts reshaping the competitive landscape. *Evidenced by:* Sets multi-year direction for a function and defends its trade-offs at the executive table with market and delivery evidence. *Scope:* a function. *Why this level:* STRAT-01 P5 ('sets the enterprise strategic direction; anticipates major shifts reshaping the competitive landscape') matches VP-level ownership of strategy that is genuinely enterprise-facing -- multi-year direction defended at the executive table with market and delivery evidence -- a real step up from M4's sub-org-scoped pattern-setting, not a repeated bar. (Rumelt, Good Strategy Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Crown Business, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M6 (= P6)** — Defines corporate-strategy practice recognized industry-wide; externally cited thought leader. *Evidenced by:* Frames the company-level bets the executive team commits to, and revises the diagnosis publicly when it changes. *Scope:* org-wide. *Why this level:* STRAT-01 P6 ('defines corporate-strategy practice recognized industry-wide; externally cited thought leader') is Rumelt's guiding-policy discipline applied at the org-wide, thesis-setting register. (Rumelt, Good Strategy Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Crown Business, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))

*Anchor:* Doerr, Measure What Matters: How Google, Bono, and the Gates Foundation Rock the World with OKRs (Portfolio/Penguin, 2018) — cascading objectives is how strategy becomes goals people can hit or miss, not just a slogan

(Doerr, Measure What Matters: How Google, Bono, and the Gates Foundation Rock the World with OKRs (Portfolio/Penguin, 2018); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))

(Doerr, Measure What Matters: How Google, Bono, and the Gates Foundation Rock the World with OKRs (Portfolio/Penguin, 2018); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M6 (= P6)** — Defines strategic-planning best practice recognized across the field; externally credible authority. *Evidenced by:* Audits the line of sight from mission to every level's goals, and publicly retires goals that no longer serve it. *Scope:* org-wide. *Why this level:* STRAT-02 P6 ('defines strategic-planning best practice recognized across the field; externally credible authority') is Doerr's cascading-objectives discipline applied at the org-wide, mission-to-goal-line register. (Doerr, Measure What Matters: How Google, Bono, and the Gates Foundation Rock the World with OKRs (Portfolio/Penguin, 2018); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))

#### Change & Organizational Design

##### Leading Change

- **M1 (= P2)** — Leads a contained team change with normal review. *Evidenced by:* Lands a team-level change by naming the why, piloting it, and following through past the initial dip. *Scope:* one team. *Why this level:* EM-12 P2 ('leads a contained team change with normal review') is Kotter's urgency-plus-follow-through discipline applied to one team's change, naming the why and piloting rather than decreeing it. (Kotter, Leading Change (Harvard Business School Press, 1996); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M3 (= P4)** — Defines change approaches others adopt and rescues stalled transformations. *Evidenced by:* Builds the coalition for a cross-team change before announcing it, recruiting early adopters and converting skeptics on the record. *Scope:* a domain (cross-team). *Why this level:* EM-12 P4 ('defines change approaches others adopt and rescues stalled transformations') is building the coalition for a cross-team change before announcing it -- adoption voluntary before it is mandatory -- Director-level. (Kotter, Leading Change (Harvard Business School Press, 1996); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M4 (= P4)** — Defines change approaches others adopt and rescues stalled transformations. *Evidenced by:* Runs multiple concurrent changes across a sub-org without exceeding its absorption capacity, with explicit reversal criteria. *Scope:* a sub-org. *Why this level:* M4 keeps P4; Senior Director scope is running multiple concurrent changes across a sub-org without exceeding its absorption capacity, with explicit reversal criteria, not a deeper change behavior. (Kotter, Leading Change (Harvard Business School Press, 1996); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M5 (= P5)** — Sets the org's transformation strategy and anticipates where change is needed next. *Evidenced by:* Leads function-wide transformation with an honest cost narrative executives sign up for, anchored in reviews and incentives. *Scope:* a function. *Why this level:* EM-12 P5 ('sets the org's transformation strategy and anticipates where change is needed next') matches VP-level ownership of function-wide transformation with an honest cost narrative executives sign up for. (Kotter, Leading Change (Harvard Business School Press, 1996); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))
- **M6 (= P6)** — Defines effective engineering transformation for the discipline and shapes external practice. *Evidenced by:* Times and frames org-wide change against market conditions, absorbing the political cost personally so teams can execute. *Scope:* org-wide. *Why this level:* EM-12 P6 ('defines effective engineering transformation for the discipline and shapes external practice') is Kotter's change model applied at the field-shaping, market-timed register. (Kotter, Leading Change (Harvard Business School Press, 1996); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))

#### Alignment & Adaptive Leadership

(Bungay, The Art of Action: How Leaders Close the Gaps between Plans, Actions and Results (Nicholas Brealey, 2011); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07))

##### Situational & Adaptive Leadership

*Anchor:* Hersey & Blanchard, Management of Organizational Behavior: Utilizing Human Resources (Prentice-Hall, 1969) — there is no single right style; the meta-skill is reading what the person, team, and moment need

| OCF | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 | M6 |

- **M1 (= P2)** — Matches direction and support to each person's readiness per task — directing, coaching, supporting, or delegating deliberately — and tells technical problems from adaptive ones. *Evidenced by:* Delegates a familiar task to a seasoned engineer and coaches a struggling new hire through the same kind of task step by step, in the same week. *Scope:* one team. *Why this level:* LI-08 P2 ('matches direction and support to each person's readiness per task ... and tells technical problems from adaptive ones') is Hersey & Blanchard's Situational Leadership model applied deliberately across one team, plus Heifetz's technical-versus-adaptive diagnosis at its most basic. (Hersey & Blanchard, Management of Organizational Behavior: Utilizing Human Resources (Prentice-Hall, 1969); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07); Heifetz, Grashow & Linsky, The Practice of Adaptive Leadership (Harvard Business Press, 2009))
- **M2 (= P3)** — Flexes skillfully across a wide range of people and hard situations without losing a grounded center; refuses to force technical fixes onto adaptive challenges and names the difference. *Evidenced by:* Refuses to force a technical fix onto an adaptive challenge on a critical team, and names the difference in the room. *Scope:* a complex / critical team. *Why this level:* LI-08 P3 ('flexes skillfully across a wide range of people and hard situations ... refuses to force technical fixes onto adaptive challenges') is applying Heifetz's diagnosis under conflicting demands on a critical team without losing a grounded center -- the competent band's hallmark. (Hersey & Blanchard, Management of Organizational Behavior: Utilizing Human Resources (Prentice-Hall, 1969); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07); Heifetz, Grashow & Linsky, The Practice of Adaptive Leadership (Harvard Business Press, 2009))
- **M3 (= P4)** — Coaches other leaders to read what a person, team, or moment needs; adapts across diverse teams while staying recognizably consistent in values. *Evidenced by:* Coaches a peer lead who defaults to hands-on rescue to recognize when a team's problem is adaptive, not technical, and to back off instead of fixing it themselves. *Scope:* a domain (cross-team). *Why this level:* LI-08 P4 ('coaches other leaders to read what a person, team, or moment needs ... adapts across diverse teams while staying recognizably consistent in values') is teaching the diagnosis, not just practicing it -- Director-level per SFIA's higher responsibility band. (Hersey & Blanchard, Management of Organizational Behavior: Utilizing Human Resources (Prentice-Hall, 1969); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07); Heifetz, Grashow & Linsky, The Practice of Adaptive Leadership (Harvard Business Press, 2009))
- **M4 (= P4)** — Coaches other leaders to read what a person, team, or moment needs; adapts across diverse teams while staying recognizably consistent in values. *Evidenced by:* Develops adaptive leadership as a discipline across a sub-org, teaching leaders to diagnose technical-versus-adaptive at scale. *Scope:* a sub-org. *Why this level:* M4 keeps P4; Senior Director scope is developing adaptive leadership as a discipline across a sub-org -- teaching leaders to diagnose technical-versus-adaptive at scale -- not a deeper personal-flexing behavior. (Hersey & Blanchard, Management of Organizational Behavior: Utilizing Human Resources (Prentice-Hall, 1969); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07); Heifetz, Grashow & Linsky, The Practice of Adaptive Leadership (Harvard Business Press, 2009))
- **M5 (= P5)** — Develops adaptive leadership as an organizational discipline — leaders diagnose technical-versus-adaptive at scale, and the leadership style changes when the situation does. *Evidenced by:* Sets the tone for adaptive, authentic leadership across a function, adjusting their own approach to what the function needs now. *Scope:* a function. *Why this level:* LI-08 P5 ('develops adaptive leadership as an organizational discipline ... the leadership style changes when the situation does') matches VP-level ownership of the tone for adaptive, authentic leadership across a function. (Hersey & Blanchard, Management of Organizational Behavior: Utilizing Human Resources (Prentice-Hall, 1969); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07); Heifetz, Grashow & Linsky, The Practice of Adaptive Leadership (Harvard Business Press, 2009))
- **M6 (= P6)** — Shapes an adaptive, grounded leadership culture across an organization — style stays plastic to conditions while values stay fixed; the approach is emulated beyond the organization. *Evidenced by:* Keeps the org's leadership style recognizable through a merger that could have imported a completely different culture. *Scope:* org-wide. *Why this level:* LI-08 P6 ('shapes an adaptive, grounded leadership culture ... style stays plastic to conditions while values stay fixed') is Heifetz's institution-level prescription for sustained adaptive work applied org-wide. (Hersey & Blanchard, Management of Organizational Behavior: Utilizing Human Resources (Prentice-Hall, 1969); GitLab Handbook, Engineering Management Job Framework (accessed 2026-07); Heifetz, Grashow & Linsky, The Practice of Adaptive Leadership (Harvard Business Press, 2009))
