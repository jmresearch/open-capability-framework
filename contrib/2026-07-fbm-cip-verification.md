# CIP cycle execution & verification

- **Proposed id:** FBM-16
- **Domain / focus area:** Food & Beverage Manufacturing / Sanitation
- **Type:** Technical
- **Description:** Executes and verifies clean-in-place cycles against time, temperature, concentration, and flow so process equipment is provably clean before it holds product again.
- **Why it's missing:** The role runs and proves CIP on closed process systems — selecting the circuit/recipe, confirming the four cleaning parameters (time, temperature, concentration, flow/coverage) were met, and verifying the result before release — a food-safety-critical sanitation craft with its own validation frame. SCM-09 Asset & maintenance management is about equipment reliability and upkeep, not proving equipment clean; WPL-02 Building operations & maintenance covers facility cleaning generically; QA-04 Risk-based quality frames quality decisions but carries none of the CIP execution/verification competence. None is sanitation- or food-safety-specific.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Runs the CIP cycle on the checklist under direction — selects the correct circuit/recipe, confirms the cycle steps run, records the time/temperature/concentration readings, and reports a short cycle or missed parameter to the lead.
- **P2 (Independent):** Executes and verifies CIP independently across a shift — runs the right circuit, confirms wash time, temperature, chemical concentration, and return flow/coverage met the recipe, and re-cleans or holds equipment when a parameter fails.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Troubleshoots CIP for the area — diagnoses low flow/coverage, temperature or concentration shortfalls, air pockets, and burst-sequence faults, proves cleaning by the verification standard (ATP/visual/rinse), recovers a failed circuit, and coaches operators on CIP verification.
- **P4 (Expert):** Sets the CIP procedure and acceptance criteria the area follows — defines the circuit recipes, the cycle time, temperature, and flow/coverage parameters, and the verification method and limits that prove each circuit clean; takes the chemical-concentration target from the cleaning-chemistry standard (owned by titration control) rather than setting it, and validates that the cycle actually removes the soil.
- **P5 (Authority):** Governs the CIP program for the department — owns the cleaning validation and verification standards across circuits, drives improvement in cleaning reliability, water/chemical/energy use, and cycle time, and defends CIP validation and records in audits.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Defines CIP execution and verification practice beyond the plant — sets cleaning-validation and verification methods other sites adopt and advances the practice in industry forums.
