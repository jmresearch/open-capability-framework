# Executive communication

- **Proposed id:** CC-06
- **Domain / focus area:** Communication & Collaboration / Communication
- **Type:** Behavioral
- **Description:** Communicates upward and to executive audiences — answer-first briefings, decision-ready framing, and composure under hostile questioning.
- **Why it's missing:** The engineering-management ladder consolidation needed a distinct row for upward/executive communication (present in all three ensemble runs). CC-01 (Written & Verbal Communication) covers general clarity but not the specific competence of winning decisions from time-poor senior audiences; PM-13 (Stakeholder & executive management) covers relationship alignment, not the communication craft itself.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Prepares status updates for leadership with guidance; leads with the headline when coached to.
- **P2 (Independent):** Reports upward answer-first with a specific ask; escalates early with options attached, so no surprise reaches leadership from someone else.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Briefs senior leaders under pressure — facts, impact, options, and a recommendation inside the first two minutes; one-pagers get forwarded unedited.
- **P4 (Expert):** Builds and lands investment cases with executive audiences — pre-wired with skeptics, sized in business terms, honest about risk; coaches others for executive rooms and runs the operating reviews an organization communicates through.
- **P5 (Authority):** Operates the executive room as a peer — advances positions, absorbs hostile questioning without defensiveness, and changes the room's decision more often than not.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Speaks for an organization's technical reality to boards, investors, and press; trusted with the messages that cannot be delegated, and organizational credibility survives the appearances.
