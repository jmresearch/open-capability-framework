# Domain & industry fluency

- **Proposed id:** PM-19
- **Domain / focus area:** Product Management / Strategy & Planning
- **Type:** Behavioral
- **Description:** Builds and maintains deep working knowledge of the product's domain — its industry structure, customer workflows, constraints, and trajectory — deeply enough to be a credible reference for customers and colleagues.
- **Why it's missing:** Three of the five sources behind the product-manager canonical role carry a distinct domain-expertise competency (Cagan's non-delegable knowledge of customer, business, and industry; the trusted fifth source's industry/operational knowledge rows). Nearest existing ids: PM-03 (competitive & product-market analysis) covers the external competitive landscape, not internal domain/workflow depth; DATA-20 (business & domain acumen) is defined around framing data work; PD-07 (strategic & commercial awareness) frames decisions in economic terms rather than domain terms. None make domain mastery itself assessable for product roles.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Learns the domain deliberately — shadows customers, reads industry sources, asks how the system actually works — and uses domain vocabulary correctly.
- **P2 (Independent):** Answers most domain questions without deferring and corrects domain errors in specs and designs.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Is the recognized domain reference for a product — customers, field teams, and engineers check facts with them — and spots domain-driven risks others miss.
- **P4 (Expert):** Connects domain knowledge across teams, catching where one roadmap violates another domain's constraints (regulatory, workflow, ecosystem).
- **P5 (Authority):** Represents an organization's domain understanding externally — industry forums, advisory boards, analyst briefings.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Anticipates domain evolution — where the industry's workflows and rules are heading — and positions portfolios for it before consensus forms.
