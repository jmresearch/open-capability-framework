# Self-awareness & learning agility

- **Proposed id:** LI-09
- **Domain / focus area:** Leadership & Influence / Personal Leadership
- **Type:** Behavioral
- **Description:** Seeks external feedback on their own behavior, knows their failure patterns, and extracts lessons from first-time situations fast enough to lead credibly in them.
- **Why it's missing:** Present in all three ensemble runs and the workbook (Eurich; Lombardo & Eichinger; Goleman). The catalog has no self-management capability: CC-04 (Feedback) covers giving/receiving feedback about work, not the metacognitive practice of studying one's own impact and retooling for unfamiliar scope.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Accepts feedback about their own behavior without defensiveness; acts on it when the change is spelled out.
- **P2 (Independent):** Asks for feedback on their own practice and changes visibly in response — "you said X, I changed Y" — keeping a running list of their own failure patterns.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Knows their failure modes under pressure and manages them in the moment; staffs deliberately against known weaknesses and seeks disconfirming input before hard calls.
- **P4 (Expert):** Runs structured input on themselves — 360s, skip-level themes — and closes the loop publicly on what changed; retools openly for first-time challenges so others copy the learning method.
- **P5 (Authority):** Compensates deliberately for the seniority feedback vacuum — cultivates truth-tellers, rewards bearers of unwelcome news about themselves, and retires their own outdated playbook when the organization outgrows it.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Institutionalizes challenge to their own judgment — red teams, protected dissent — and manages how their moods and offhand comments get amplified across an organization.
