# Opportunity mapping & framing

- **Proposed id:** PM-15
- **Domain / focus area:** Product Management / Discovery & Research
- **Type:** Behavioral
- **Description:** Structures discovered customer needs into an explicit opportunity space — mapped, sized, and compared — so solution choices trace to the most valuable problem rather than the first idea.
- **Why it's missing:** The product-manager canonical role (roles/product-manager) carries an opportunity-mapping competency present in all five consolidation sources (opportunity solution trees per Torres, opportunity assessment in the trusted fifth source). Nearest existing ids: PM-01 (product discovery & customer research) covers uncovering and validating needs but not structuring the option space into a comparable map; PM-04 (product strategy & positioning) consumes the map but does not build it; PM-05 (roadmap & portfolio planning) sequences chosen work rather than framing unchosen opportunity. None cover the mapping discipline itself.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Places research findings onto an existing opportunity map correctly, distinguishing opportunities from solutions with coaching.
- **P2 (Independent):** Builds and maintains the opportunity map for their area, sizing and pruning branches against evidence without prompting.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Structures messy, contested problem spaces into maps that make trade-offs explicit, and defends why one opportunity is pursued over its siblings.
- **P4 (Expert):** Reconciles overlapping opportunity maps across teams, exposing duplicate bets and orphaned opportunities no single team could see.
- **P5 (Authority):** Makes opportunity mapping an organization's default synthesis method; investment reviews open from the mapped opportunity space.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Shapes how organizations represent customer opportunity at portfolio level; their framing methods are cited and reused beyond their own organization.
