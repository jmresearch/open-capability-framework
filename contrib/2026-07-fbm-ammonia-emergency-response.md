# Ammonia emergency response & readiness

- **Proposed id:** FBM-25
- **Domain / focus area:** Food & Beverage Manufacturing / Process Safety
- **Type:** Behavioral
- **Description:** Preparing for and leading the response to an ammonia release or process-safety emergency — detection, evacuation and shelter, notification, and incident command — so people are protected and the release is controlled.
- **Why it's missing:** An anhydrous-ammonia release is a life-safety and community event; the dairy-plant role must have a rehearsed emergency-response program and someone able to take command when it happens. The nearest catalog capabilities do not cover it: WPL-08 Environmental health & safety is general workplace safety, not leading an incident-command response to a toxic-chemical release with agency notification; FBM-24 PSM/RMP owns the covered-process program and prevention, not the live response and readiness; QA-04 Risk-based quality is unrelated. None is chemical-emergency-response specific.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Knows the ammonia alarm, the evacuation and assembly points, and their own role in the emergency plan; on a release recognizes it, evacuates or shelters as trained, and reports to the muster point and accounts for themselves.
- **P2 (Independent):** Acts correctly and independently in a release — takes the trained initial actions (alarm, isolate if safe, evacuate the area, don assigned PPE), gives clear information to the responders, and knows the boundary of what they may and may not do without the response team.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Leads the response at the scene for the shift — directs evacuation and accountability, isolates the process, briefs arriving responders, and runs their part of the plan calmly, and coaches the crew through drills so they perform under real conditions.
- **P4 (Expert):** Runs incident command for an ammonia emergency at the plant — sizes up the release, decides shelter-versus-evacuate and isolation, initiates the required agency notifications, and directs the response — and sets the response procedures and drill standard others follow.
- **P5 (Authority):** Governs emergency-response readiness plant-wide — the emergency-response plan, drill and exercise program, mutual-aid and agency relationships, detection and notification systems, and the after-action process that closes gaps — for the covered process.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Sets ammonia-emergency-response practice beyond the site — command models, community-coordination and drill standards, and readiness approaches other plants, mutual-aid partners, and the industry adopt.
