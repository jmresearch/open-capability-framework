# Technical credibility

- **Proposed id:** LI-06
- **Domain / focus area:** Leadership & Influence / Technical Leadership
- **Type:** Behavioral
- **Description:** Retains enough hands-on technical depth to earn engineers' trust, ask the questions that change designs, and judge technical work without doing it.
- **Why it's missing:** Central to the engineering-leadership workbook source and implicit in every manager ladder: leaders who cannot engage technically lose the room. LI-03 (Technical Influence & Direction) covers shaping direction; SWE-* capabilities cover doing the work. Neither covers the retained-depth competence of judging and probing work credibly while someone else authors it.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Follows the team's technical discussions and asks clarifying questions; relies on others to evaluate design quality.
- **P2 (Independent):** Understands the systems in scope well enough to review designs, probe trade-offs, and triage incidents — earns technical respect without taking the keyboard.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Maintains depth across a complex system's stack and hardest problems; a credible thought-partner to the most senior engineers, pairing them with the context they need.
- **P4 (Expert):** Retains broad credibility across a multi-team domain — credible on its hardest cross-cutting technical questions, current enough to ask the question that changes a design.
- **P5 (Authority):** Sustains the credibility to guide an organization's technology direction; translates architectural risk into business terms without distortion, and senior technologists accept the judgment.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Sustains credibility across an entire technology landscape; their technical judgment is trusted at every altitude because it survives contact with detail.
