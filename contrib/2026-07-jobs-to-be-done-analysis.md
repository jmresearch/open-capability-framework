# Jobs-to-be-done analysis

- **Proposed id:** PM-17
- **Domain / focus area:** Product Management / Discovery & Research
- **Type:** Behavioral
- **Description:** Models what customers hire products to accomplish — the circumstance, the progress sought, and the competing alternatives (including workarounds and non-consumption) — to predict adoption and define product boundaries.
- **Why it's missing:** Three of the four blind runs behind the product-manager canonical role carry a distinct jobs-to-be-done competency (Christensen). Nearest existing ids: PM-02 (user persona & journey modeling) models who the customer is and their experience path — jobs analysis deliberately replaces the who with the causal why-hire; PM-03 (competitive & product-market analysis) covers named competitors, while JTBD surfaces non-obvious competition for the same job. Neither makes causal job modeling assessable.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Restates a feature request as the job the customer is hiring it for — circumstance and progress sought — with coaching.
- **P2 (Independent):** Maps the main jobs in their area from interview evidence, identifying what customers currently hire and where those hires fail.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Anchors a product's definition and boundaries in its core job, using the job to rule scope in or out and to identify non-obvious competitors.
- **P4 (Expert):** Aligns job maps across teams into one view of customer progress, exposing where customers must stitch products together to finish one job.
- **P5 (Authority):** Uses jobs analysis to shape organization-level portfolio choices — which jobs are served end-to-end, which are exited.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Frames markets and strategy in jobs terms that reveal disruption threats early; their jobs analyses influence practice beyond their organization.
