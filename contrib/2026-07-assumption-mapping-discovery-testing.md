# Assumption mapping & discovery testing

- **Proposed id:** PM-18
- **Domain / focus area:** Product Management / Discovery & Research
- **Type:** Behavioral
- **Description:** Names and ranks the assumptions behind a product bet — desirability, viability, feasibility, usability — and designs the cheapest decisive pre-build tests (prototypes, fake doors, concierge runs) to validate or kill them.
- **Why it's missing:** The product-manager canonical role carries three source-backed competencies in this territory (assumption mapping & risk framing; assumption testing & experiments; prototype & solution testing), present across all five consolidation sources. Nearest existing ids: PM-11 (product experimentation & A/B testing) covers controlled experiments on live traffic — a different instrument with different statistics; PM-01 (product discovery & customer research) covers uncovering and validating problems, not risk-ranking solution bets or choosing test fidelity. Neither makes pre-build validation discipline assessable. This capability intentionally spans mapping and testing (one practice per Torres and Bland & Osterwalder); it carries three ladder rows in roles/product-manager and is a candidate for a future split if more roles need finer grain.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Lists the assumptions behind a proposed feature, sorts them by risk with coaching, and runs a prescribed simple test, logging what was learned.
- **P2 (Independent):** Identifies the riskiest assumption before build and matches the test type to it — prototype, painted door, concierge — with pass/fail defined in advance.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Surfaces assumptions others treat as facts, designs test sequences that retire the most risk per unit effort, and kills initiatives whose load-bearing assumptions fail.
- **P4 (Expert):** Standardizes how multiple teams express and rank risk, and rejects validation theater — tests that can only confirm, never falsify — before major commitments.
- **P5 (Authority):** Builds discovery-testing capability into an organization's investment process — funding follows tested assumptions, and the cost of a first test drops to hours.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Structures bets too big to A/B as staged, falsifiable commitments; their validation doctrine is cited and adopted beyond their organization.
