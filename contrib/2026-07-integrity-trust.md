# Integrity & trust

- **Proposed id:** LI-11
- **Domain / focus area:** Leadership & Influence / Personal Leadership
- **Type:** Behavioral
- **Description:** Earns and keeps trust through consistency and courage — keeping commitments, telling unwelcome truths, and holding the ethical line when it is costly.
- **Why it's missing:** Present in all three ensemble runs and the workbook (Mayer, Davis & Schoorman; ACM Code of Ethics; Covey). The catalog's nearest ids are RISK-09 (Ethics & conduct program management — running a compliance program, not personal integrity) and SEC-16 (adversarial ethics, security-specific). Personal trustworthiness as a ratable behavioral capability is absent.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Keeps commitments with reminders; is honest when asked directly; escalates ethical concerns to others.
- **P2 (Independent):** Keeps commitments or renegotiates before the deadline, never after; says the same thing in the room and out of it, owns mistakes unprompted, and reports true status when the truth is unwelcome.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Makes the unpopular-but-right call under pressure and absorbs the cost personally; protects the person who raised the flag and handles sensitive information impeccably.
- **P4 (Expert):** Serves as the honest broker across teams — disputing parties accept their account of the facts — and carries uncomfortable systemic truths to the room that can fix them.
- **P5 (Authority):** Builds mechanisms that make integrity cheap at scale — ethics review in design, safe escalation with follow-through, fairness systems whose losers affirm the process — and stops launches on ethical grounds with the business case for trust.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Holds an organization's line when the profitable path and the right path diverge — the hard disclosure, the walked-away revenue — and those moments become the story the culture tells about itself.
