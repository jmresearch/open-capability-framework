# Onboarding & team formation

- **Proposed id:** EM-13
- **Domain / focus area:** Engineering Management / People Management
- **Type:** Behavioral
- **Description:** Ramps new people to productivity fast and leads teams through formation stages — launches, merges, and resets — deliberately rather than enduring them.
- **Why it's missing:** Sourced from one ensemble run (Tuckman anchor). EM-04 (Team design & org structuring) covers structure, HR-14 covers lifecycle administration; neither covers the manager behavior of running onboarding and reading/steering a team's forming-storming-norming-performing stage.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Follows an existing onboarding checklist for new hires; escalates ramp problems.
- **P2 (Independent):** Runs a 30/60/90 onboarding with named buddies and early wins; new hires ship in their first weeks and say so in check-ins.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Reads a team's formation stage and intervenes on it — resets norms after a merge, names storming in the room instead of managing it by email.
- **P4 (Expert):** Builds the onboarding and team-launch playbook multiple teams use; teams start faster because of materials and rituals they created, and stands up whole new teams repeatedly.
- **P5 (Authority):** Designs how an organization absorbs step-change growth — acquisitions, new sites, doubled headcount — without culture dilution; integration plans carry named cultural mechanisms.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Makes team formation an organization-wide capability — new groups spin up predictably without heroics — and audits that the machinery still works.
