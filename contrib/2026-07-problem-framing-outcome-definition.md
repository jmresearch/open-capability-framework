# Problem framing & outcome definition

- **Proposed id:** PM-16
- **Domain / focus area:** Product Management / Discovery & Research
- **Type:** Behavioral
- **Description:** Frames incoming requests and observations as customer problems tied to measurable outcomes — separating the problem from the proposed solution before work is committed.
- **Why it's missing:** All five sources of the product-manager canonical role carry a problem-framing competency (problem statements, outcome-over-output framing per Perri). Nearest existing ids: PM-01 (discovery & research) covers researching problems, not the written framing discipline; PM-07 (requirement & specification definition) is downstream — translating an already-framed problem into buildable requirements; PD-03 (dealing with ambiguity) is a general behavioral capability about making progress, not the craft of outcome-first problem definition. None make "reframes a solution-shaped request into a measurable customer outcome" assessable.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Writes a one-page problem statement naming the user, the struggle, and the evidence, with review; separates problem from requested feature when prompted.
- **P2 (Independent):** Frames work as outcomes the team can move rather than outputs to ship, and rewrites incoming feature requests into the underlying problem before triage.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Sets the outcome a product is accountable for and keeps the team pointed at it, publicly re-framing efforts that drift into output-counting.
- **P4 (Expert):** Arbitrates problem framing across teams — spotting duplicated or conflicting framings — and re-cuts problem boundaries drawn along org lines instead of customer lines.
- **P5 (Authority):** Defines an organization's outcome architecture — how team-level outcomes ladder to organizational results — and retires vanity outcomes.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Frames an organization's hardest, ownerless problems into tractable outcome statements executives fund; their framing practice shapes the discipline externally.
