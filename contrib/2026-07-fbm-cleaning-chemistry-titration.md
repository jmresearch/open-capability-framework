# Cleaning chemistry & titration control

- **Proposed id:** FBM-17
- **Domain / focus area:** Food & Beverage Manufacturing / Sanitation
- **Type:** Technical
- **Description:** Controls cleaning-chemical concentration by titration and matches chemistry, temperature, and contact time to the soil so cleaning is effective and repeatable.
- **Why it's missing:** The role controls the chemistry behind cleaning — titrating wash and sanitizer solutions to concentration and matching alkalinity/acidity, temperature, and contact time to the soil type (protein, mineral, fat, biofilm) — a hands-on food-plant sanitation craft distinct from running the cleaning cycle. SCM-09 Asset & maintenance management does not address cleaning chemistry; WPL-08 Environmental health & safety covers chemical handling from a worker-safety angle, not concentration control for cleaning efficacy; QA-04 Risk-based quality is decision-framing, not the titration competence. None is sanitation- or food-safety-specific.

## P1–P6 behavioral profile

- **P1 (Assisted):** Performs the titration on the checklist under direction — draws the sample, runs the titration to endpoint, records the concentration, and reports an out-of-range result rather than releasing the solution.
- **P2 (Independent):** Controls cleaning-chemical concentration independently across a shift — titrates wash and sanitizer solutions, adjusts to the target concentration, and matches chemistry, temperature, and contact time to the standard cleaning task.
- **P3 (Proficient):** Troubleshoots cleaning chemistry for the area — diagnoses why a soil isn't clearing, adjusts chemistry, temperature, and contact time to the soil (protein, mineral, fat, biofilm), corrects drifting titrations and dosing faults, and coaches operators on titration technique.
- **P4 (Expert):** Sets the cleaning-chemistry procedure and concentration limits the area follows — defines the chemical selection, target concentrations, titration methods, and time/temperature parameters matched to each soil that others run to, and qualifies changes to chemistry.
- **P5 (Authority):** Governs cleaning chemistry for the department — owns the chemical program and concentration/titration standards, drives improvement in cleaning effectiveness, chemical cost, and safety, and defends chemical-control and handling records in audits.
- **P6 (Pioneer):** Defines cleaning-chemistry and titration-control practice beyond the plant — sets soil-matched chemistry and concentration-verification methods other sites adopt and advances the practice in industry forums.
